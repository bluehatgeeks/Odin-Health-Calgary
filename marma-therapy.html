Ayurvedic Origin · Precision Application

What Is Marma Therapy?

The word marma derives from the Sanskrit mri — death or injury. Points were catalogued first as sites of danger, not healing. Knowing where the body is most vulnerable is the same knowledge that directs precise therapeutic support.

The Sushruta Samhita (1000–600 BCE) defined 107 marma points — each a confluence of muscle, blood vessels, ligaments, bone, and joints — and organized them across the body's three major regions. Sushruta considered knowledge of the marmas essential to surgical safety itself.

In practice, marma points function as access nodes to the body's regulatory channels. When these junctions become congested through injury, chronic stress, or structural misuse, dysfunction follows. Marma therapy intervenes with targeted pressure or vibration to restore flow. At Odin Lab, session design is informed by your health intake, not a generic sequence.

The Science of Therapeutic Frequency

When a struck tuning fork contacts the body, it transmits mechanical vibration at its calibrated frequency. Biological tissues — bone, fascia, fluid, nervous tissue — each carry characteristic vibrational properties, and precise mechanical input at the right frequency can influence their state and function through sympathetic resonance.

128 Hz is the established clinical standard for vibration sense testing. In therapeutic contexts it is associated with nitric oxide release, mechanoreceptor activation, and gate-control modulation of pain signaling. 432 Hz is used in therapeutic practice for nervous system downregulation, associated in preliminary research with alpha brainwave support; 528 Hz has been studied in preliminary research in relation to stress biomarker reduction.

Peer-reviewed vibroacoustic therapy research demonstrates measurable autonomic effects: increased parasympathetic activity, reduced cortisol-pattern EEG arousal (Fooks et al., 2024), and consistent pain score reductions at 40 Hz across 20 clinical studies (Kantor et al., 2022). The vagus nerve is the primary mechanism — sustained low-frequency vibration produces measurable increases in heart rate variability.
