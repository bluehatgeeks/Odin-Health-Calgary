Core Protocols

Core Supplement Categories

Six foundational supplement classes — each with characterized mechanisms and peer-reviewed clinical evidence supporting targeted therapeutic use.

Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA/DHA)

Mechanism

EPA and DHA integrate into cell membranes, modulate prostaglandin and cytokine synthesis, and activate anti-inflammatory transcription pathways (NF-κB suppression, SPM production). DHA additionally supports mitochondrial biogenesis and fatty acid oxidation, while EPA inhibits neuroinflammation via microglial polarization.

Conditions Supported

Cardiovascular disease risk reduction, systemic inflammation, metabolic syndrome, neuroinflammation, mood dysregulation, and cognitive decline prevention.

Sherratt SCR et al. "N-3 Fatty Acids (EPA and DHA) and Cardiovascular Health — Updated Review of Mechanisms and Clinical Outcomes." Cardiovascular Research, 2023. Meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=135,291) found significant reductions in MACE, cardiovascular death, and myocardial infarction.

Magnesium Glycinate

Mechanism

Magnesium is a required cofactor in over 600 enzymatic reactions, including ATP synthesis, DNA replication, and neuromuscular signaling. It functions as a physiological calcium channel antagonist, supports nitric oxide bioavailability, and regulates insulin receptor tyrosine kinase activity. Deficiency is common and frequently undetected because serum levels do not reflect intracellular status.

Conditions Supported

Hypertension, insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, migraine frequency, sleep quality, depression, osteoporosis, and stress-related adrenal dysregulation.

Barbagallo M et al. "Magnesium: Health Effects, Deficiency Burden, and Future Public Health Directions." Nutrients, 2025. Hypomagnesemia reported in 14–48% of type 2 diabetes patients; supplementation at 250–400 mg/day shows efficacy across metabolic and neurological domains.

Vitamin D3 / K2

Mechanism

1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3 (calcitriol) binds vitamin D receptors (VDR) expressed on immune cells, regulating both innate and adaptive immunity. It shifts immune response toward regulatory phenotypes — increasing T-regulatory cells, suppressing Th17 — and modulates cytokine production, gut barrier integrity, and microbiota composition through VDR signaling.

Conditions Supported

Immune dysregulation, autoimmune conditions (SLE, multiple sclerosis, type 1 diabetes, Crohn's disease), recurrent infections, seasonal mood disruption, and bone density loss.

Sîrbe C et al. "An Update on the Effects of Vitamin D on the Immune System and Autoimmune Diseases." Int J Mol Sciences, 2022. VITAL trial demonstrated lower incidence of new-onset autoimmune disease with 2,000 IU/day vitamin D3.

B-Complex (Methylated Forms)

Mechanism

B vitamins — particularly methylfolate (5-MTHF), methylcobalamin (B12), and pyridoxal-5-phosphate (B6) — serve as essential cofactors in one-carbon metabolism: a network of reactions critical for DNA synthesis, methylation, neurotransmitter production, and homocysteine recycling. Methylated forms bypass conversion steps impaired by the common MTHFR gene variant (C677T, A1298C), ensuring cellular delivery of active nutrients.

Conditions Supported

Fatigue and low energy, elevated homocysteine, cognitive fog, mood disorders, MTHFR-related methylation impairment, and cardiovascular risk reduction.

Carboni L. "Active Folate Versus Folic Acid: The Role of 5-MTHF in Human Health." Integrative Medicine (Encinitas), 2022;21(3):36–41. Methylfolate (400 µg Quatrefolic) combined with B6 and B12 reduced homocysteine more effectively than 5 mg/day folic acid in a 104-patient trial.

Zinc Bisglycinate

Mechanism

Zinc is an essential cofactor for immune cell development (T cells, B cells, NK cells, neutrophils, and macrophages), cytokine regulation, and collagen synthesis. It modulates macrophage polarization between pro-inflammatory (M1) and tissue-repair (M2) phenotypes, supports T-regulatory cell proliferation, and is required for thyroid hormone synthesis.

Conditions Supported

Immune deficiency, wound healing impairment, recurrent infection, skin conditions, thyroid dysfunction, and testosterone regulation.

Lin P-H et al. "Zinc in Wound Healing Modulation." Nutrients, 2018. Meta-analysis of five clinical trials found zinc treatment significantly associated with improved ulcer healing outcomes (MD: 1.41; 95% CI 1.04–1.92; p=0.03).

Multi-Strain Probiotics

Mechanism

Probiotics restore microbial diversity, produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) that support colonocyte metabolism and epithelial barrier integrity, modulate immune tone via gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), and communicate with the central nervous system through the gut-brain axis (vagal signaling, serotonin precursor production).

Conditions Supported

Irritable bowel syndrome, antibiotic-associated diarrhea, inflammatory bowel disease (especially ulcerative colitis), mood disorders, cognitive performance, and immune dysregulation.

Huang F et al. "Exploring the gut microbiome: probiotics, prebiotics, synbiotics, and postbiotics as key players in human health." PMC, 2024. 8-week double-blind RCT of a multi-strain probiotic significantly alleviated core GI symptoms and improved psychosocial wellbeing in adults with functional bowel disorders.

Herbal Medicine

Botanical Spotlights

Four botanicals with robust clinical trial evidence, well-characterized bioactive mechanisms, and a safety profile that supports long-term practitioner-supervised use.

Ashwagandha

Withania somnifera

Ashwagandha's withanolides modulate the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, suppressing stress-driven cortisol overproduction while supporting thyroid receptor sensitivity. It acts as an adaptogen — normalizing physiological stress response rather than simply sedating it. It also exhibits anti-inflammatory effects through NF-κB pathway inhibition and supports mitochondrial function.

Top Evidence-Backed Uses
  1. 01 Stress and anxiety reduction — Multiple double-blind RCTs demonstrate significant reductions in PSS, DASS, and GHQ-28 stress scores vs. placebo over 6–8 weeks
  2. 02 Cortisol normalization — KSM-66 extract (600 mg/day) demonstrated a 27.9% reduction in serum cortisol in a placebo-controlled trial of stressed adults
  3. 03 Thyroid support — A randomized placebo-controlled trial found significant reductions in TSH and increases in fT3 and fT4 at 600 mg/day; monitored use recommended in pre-existing thyroid conditions

Chandrasekhar K et al. Indian J Psychological Medicine, 2012. PMC3573577. KSM-66 cortisol/stress trial, n=64. Verma N et al. Phytotherapy Research, 2025. 12-month safety confirmation, no adverse organ effects.

Rhodiola Rosea

Rhodiola rosea

Rhodiola's primary bioactives — salidroside and rosavins — inhibit monoamine oxidase (MAO-A and MAO-B), prolonging the activity of dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, and acetylcholine in the CNS. This preserves cognitive function under stress, supports prefrontal cortex activity, and reduces the neurochemical depletion associated with sustained mental effort.

Top Evidence-Backed Uses
  1. 01 Mental fatigue reduction — Double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in 56 physicians showed statistically significant improvement in mental performance (Fatigue Index) during night duty
  2. 02 Cognitive performance under stress — RCT of 161 cadets demonstrated a pronounced antifatigue effect (AFI score) after a single dose of standardized SHR-5 vs. placebo
  3. 03 Burnout and stress-induced exhaustion — Multiple clinical trials support reduced burnout scores in individuals with stress-related fatigue syndrome; favorable safety profile at 200–600 mg/day

Darbinyan V et al. Phytomedicine, 2000;7(5):365–371. Shevtsov VA et al. Phytomedicine, 2003;10(2–3):95–105. Two foundational SHR-5 standardized extract RCTs establishing anti-fatigue evidence.

Berberine

Berberis aristata / Coptis chinensis

Berberine activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), the cellular energy-sensing enzyme, increasing glucose uptake in skeletal muscle, suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis, and promoting lipid oxidation. It also upregulates insulin receptor expression, reduces reactive oxygen species contributing to insulin resistance, and modulates the gut microbiome toward SCFA-producing species associated with improved glycemic control.

Top Evidence-Backed Uses
  1. 01 Blood glucose reduction — Meta-analysis of 37 RCTs (n=3,048) found berberine significantly reduced fasting plasma glucose (WMD: −0.82 mmol/L), HbA1c (WMD: −0.63%), and 2-hour postprandial glucose
  2. 02 Insulin resistance improvement — Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR reduced by 28.1% and 44.7% respectively (p<0.001) in clinical studies; no hypoglycemia risk as effect is conditional on hyperglycemia
  3. 03 Dyslipidemia and metabolic syndrome — Meta-analyses confirm improvements in LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and blood pressure across 12+ randomized placebo-controlled trials

Liang Y et al. Clinical Therapeutics, 2023. PMID: 38016844. Umbrella meta-analysis. Yin J et al. Metabolism, 2008. PMC2410097. Foundational 3-month RCT establishing berberine comparable to metformin for glycemic endpoints.

Lion's Mane

Hericium erinaceus

Lion's Mane contains hericenones (fruiting body) and erinacines (mycelium), both of which stimulate endogenous synthesis of Nerve Growth Factor (NGF) and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF). Erinacines cross the blood-brain barrier and have been shown to promote hippocampal neurogenesis, reduce amyloid-β plaque deposition in animal models, and attenuate neuroinflammatory markers (IL-6, TGFβ1, GFAP).

Top Evidence-Backed Uses
  1. 01 Mild cognitive impairment — 16-week randomized placebo-controlled trial in 30 individuals with MCI demonstrated significant improvement in cognitive test performance vs. placebo (improvement reversed after discontinuation)
  2. 02 Cognitive processing speed — 2023 double-blind pilot RCT (n=41, healthy adults aged 18–45) showed significantly faster Stroop task performance at 60 minutes post-dose; 28-day use showed trend toward reduced stress
  3. 03 Neuroprotection and neuroinflammation — Animal studies show reduced dopaminergic cell loss in Parkinson's models and prevention of age-related cognitive decline with 8-month supplementation in aged mice

Mori K et al. Biomedical Research, 2019. PMID: 31413233. 16-week RCT in MCI patients. Docherty S et al. Nutrients, 2023;15(22):4842. PMC10675414. Double-blind pilot RCT, healthy adults.

How We Work

Your Supplementation Protocol

A structured three-phase approach: assess your actual status, restore identified deficiencies, then optimize for performance and longevity — with objective retesting throughout.

1

Assess

  • Comprehensive biomarker panel: 25-OH vitamin D, RBC magnesium, ferritin, B12, zinc, omega-3 index, hsCRP, homocysteine, fasting glucose, HbA1c, insulin, lipid panel
  • Detailed health history intake: symptoms, diet, stress load, sleep quality, medications, and prior supplementation
  • Identification of functional deficiencies — suboptimal nutrients within conventional lab ranges that functional thresholds distinguish
  • Genetic context review where relevant: MTHFR status, COMT variants, and methylation-related polymorphisms affecting nutrient metabolism
  • Gut health and digestive function assessment: absorption capacity, microbiome balance indicators, and bowel function history
2

Restore

  • Targeted repletion of identified deficiencies using bioavailable, practitioner-grade forms: magnesium glycinate, methylfolate, methylcobalamin, cholecalciferol D3
  • Gut repair protocol where indicated: mucosal support (L-glutamine, zinc carnosine), probiotic reseeding, and removal of inflammatory dietary triggers
  • Foundational support established: omega-3 repletion, B-complex methylation support, and adrenal adaptogen introduction
  • Retesting at 8–12 weeks to confirm repletion and adjust dosing based on objective biomarker response
  • Symptom tracking alongside lab data to correlate subjective improvement with physiological change
3

Optimize

  • Shift from deficiency correction to performance, resilience, and longevity enhancement
  • Introduction of targeted botanicals and nootropics aligned with individual goals: cognitive performance (Lion's Mane, Rhodiola), hormonal balance (Ashwagandha), or metabolic optimization (Berberine, Chromium, ALA)
  • Mitochondrial support protocols for sustained energy: CoQ10 (ubiquinol form), NMN/NR, PQQ
  • Maintenance dosing calibrated to sustained biomarker targets, not fixed product regimens
  • Ongoing monitoring cadence: quarterly or semi-annual panels to ensure optimization is maintained and protocol adjusted as physiology changes
Our Standards

Quality Standards We Require

The supplement industry is largely unregulated. We apply three non-negotiable criteria to every compound we recommend — because the difference in what your body actually receives is material.

Third-Party Testing (NSF, USP, Informed Sport)

Dietary supplements are not FDA-approved before reaching the market, meaning manufacturer claims are self-reported and unverified unless independently tested. Third-party certification programs — NSF Certified for Sport, USP Verified, and Informed Sport — test finished products for label accuracy, contaminant absence, and freedom from 270+ substances banned by major sports anti-doping agencies. Certified products undergo batch-level testing, not just formulation approval, providing assurance that what is on the label is in the bottle at the stated dose.

Bioavailable Forms

Not all nutrient forms are equally usable by the body. Synthetic forms — such as folic acid (B9), cyanocobalamin (B12), and magnesium oxide — require enzymatic conversion steps that are impaired in significant portions of the population due to genetic variants like MTHFR, or are poorly absorbed compared to active alternatives. Practitioner-grade protocols prioritize bioavailable forms: methylfolate (5-MTHF) over folic acid, methylcobalamin over cyanocobalamin, magnesium glycinate or citrate over oxide, and ubiquinol CoQ10 over ubiquinone. This distinction determines whether a supplement actually reaches target tissues in functional concentrations.

Practitioner-Grade vs. Retail

Retail supplements sold through mass-market channels are formulated to price points, not clinical outcomes — often using lower-cost synthetic forms, inadequate doses, and minimal quality oversight beyond GMP labeling requirements. Practitioner-grade supplements are produced by manufacturers that supply licensed clinicians, are subject to stricter quality controls, use clinically validated doses drawn from peer-reviewed research, and typically undergo third-party verification. The difference is not branding — it is a material distinction in what your body actually receives and can use.
