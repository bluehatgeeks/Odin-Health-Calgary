Six nutrients. Measurable outcomes. Only if you actually need them.

These aren't recommendations based on general wellness trends. They're compounds with human trial data behind specific endpoints — and we only use them when your labs confirm a real gap.

Omega-3 Fatty Acids (EPA/DHA)

Why it works

If your omega-3 index is low, your triglycerides are higher than they need to be, your inflammatory load is harder to manage, and your cardiovascular risk is elevated — and most people don't know their index. EPA and DHA remodel cell membranes, shift eicosanoid balance toward resolution, and suppress inflammatory transcription. A meta-analysis of 14 RCTs covering 135,291 patients showed significant reductions in cardiovascular death and myocardial infarction. That's the evidence. The question is whether you're actually deficient — which is a lab question, not a guess.

What changes on your labs

Cardiometabolic risk reduction, high triglycerides, chronic inflammatory load, metabolic syndrome–pattern physiology, mood stabilization support, and resilient cognition as you age.

Sherratt SCR et al. "N-3 Fatty Acids (EPA and DHA) and Cardiovascular Health — Updated Review of Mechanisms and Clinical Outcomes." Cardiovascular Research, 2023. Meta-analysis of 14 RCTs (n=135,291) found significant reductions in MACE, cardiovascular death, and myocardial infarction.

Magnesium Glycinate

Why it works

Magnesium is involved in over 600 enzymatic reactions. ATP synthesis, DNA repair, neuromuscular signaling, insulin receptor function, nitric oxide tone — all of it depends on adequate magnesium. Between 14 and 48% of type 2 diabetes patients are low on labs. Most general practitioners don't test it. Standard serum magnesium is a poor proxy for intracellular status — you can look normal on a basic panel and be functionally depleted. We test it properly.

What changes on your labs

Blood pressure support, insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes–related physiology, migraine burden, sleep architecture, mood stabilization, bone density preservation, and HPA-axis–linked tension.

Barbagallo M et al. "Magnesium: Health Effects, Deficiency Burden, and Future Public Health Directions." Nutrients, 2025. Hypomagnesemia reported in 14–48% of type 2 diabetes patients; supplementation at 250–400 mg/day shows efficacy across metabolic and neurological domains.

Vitamin D3 / K2

Why it works

Vitamin D deficiency is endemic in northern climates. Calgary sits at 51° north. If you haven't tested in the last year, you're guessing. Calcitriol binds VDR receptors on immune cells, shifts immunity toward regulatory phenotypes, and modulates cytokine production — including the overactive inflammatory response seen in autoimmune conditions. The VITAL trial showed lower incidence of new-onset autoimmune disease at 2,000 IU/day. The right dose is the one that gets your 25-OH to an optimal range, not a fixed number off a label.

What changes on your labs

Immune imbalance, autoimmune–spectrum monitoring alongside specialists, infection recurrence, seasonal mood dips, and bone-mineral preservation with appropriate cofactors.

Sîrbe C et al. "An Update on the Effects of Vitamin D on the Immune System and Autoimmune Diseases." Int J Mol Sciences, 2022. VITAL trial demonstrated lower incidence of new-onset autoimmune disease with 2,000 IU/day vitamin D3.

B-Complex (Methylated Forms)

Why it works

If you have an MTHFR variant — and roughly 40% of people do — standard folic acid does almost nothing. Your body can't convert it to the active form fast enough. Methylfolate (5-MTHF) bypasses that bottleneck entirely. Combined with methylcobalamin (B12) and P-5-P (B6), it drives the one-carbon metabolism network: DNA synthesis, neurotransmitter production, homocysteine clearance. High homocysteine is a cardiovascular and neurological risk factor we can move — with the right form, at the right dose, confirmed by retesting.

What changes on your labs

Fatigue and afternoon crashes, elevated homocysteine, brain fog, mood lability, genotype-informed methylation support, and cardiovascular risk factors tied to methylation strain.

Carboni L. "Active Folate Versus Folic Acid: The Role of 5-MTHF in Human Health." Integrative Medicine (Encinitas), 2022;21(3):36–41. Methylfolate (400 µg Quatrefolic) combined with B6 and B12 reduced homocysteine more effectively than 5 mg/day folic acid in a 104-patient trial.

Zinc Bisglycinate

Why it works

Zinc is required for immune cell maturation, cytokine regulation, wound repair, and thyroid hormone synthesis. It's also commonly suboptimal — especially in plant-forward diets, high-stress physiology, and anyone on long-term proton pump inhibitors. The difference between "within range" and "optimal" on a zinc panel is often the difference between an immune system that responds and one that lingers. We test plasma zinc, not serum, and we dose to a functional target.

What changes on your labs

Immune sluggishness, slow wound healing, recurrent infection, inflammatory skin patterns, thyroid optimization support, and androgen balance where zinc insufficiency contributes.

Lin P-H et al. "Zinc in Wound Healing Modulation." Nutrients, 2018. Meta-analysis of five clinical trials found zinc treatment significantly associated with improved ulcer healing outcomes (MD: 1.41; 95% CI 1.04–1.92; p=0.03).

Multi-Strain Probiotics

Why it works

Probiotics are one of the most over-purchased and under-targeted supplements in existence. Most people buy a random multi-strain product and take it indefinitely with no clear indication. We use probiotics when the clinical picture points to gut-immune dysregulation — IBS patterns, antibiotic aftermath, inflammatory bowel involvement, or mood-gut signaling issues. The strains matter. The dose matters. The indication matters. Without a reason, you're just feeding your gut at random.

What changes on your labs

Irritable bowel patterns, antibiotic-associated diarrhea, inflammatory bowel support (often alongside gastroenterology care), stress–gut loops, foggy cognition linked to GI dysfunction, and immune reactivity with intestinal contributions.

Huang F et al. "Exploring the gut microbiome: probiotics, prebiotics, synbiotics, and postbiotics as key players in human health." PMC, 2024. 8-week double-blind RCT of a multi-strain probiotic significantly alleviated core GI symptoms and improved psychosocial wellbeing in adults with functional bowel disorders.

Clinical botanicals — not folklore

Four botanicals with human trial data. Not anecdote. Not tradition. Trials.

We use botanicals that have been through randomized, controlled trials measuring endpoints you can feel and test — cortisol, HbA1c, cognitive scores, fatigue indices. If the evidence isn't there, neither are we.

Ashwagandha

Withania somnifera

Ashwagandha works. That's the short version. The longer version: KSM-66 extract at 600 mg/day reduced serum cortisol by 27.9% in a placebo-controlled trial of stressed adults. Multiple double-blind RCTs show significant reductions in PSS and DASS stress scores within 6–8 weeks. The mechanism is HPA-axis modulation — withanolides tune cortisol output and thyroid receptor sensitivity without sedating you. It normalizes stress physiology. That's different from suppressing it.

What the trials showed
  1. 01 Stress and anxiety reduction — Multiple double-blind RCTs demonstrate significant reductions in PSS, DASS, and GHQ-28 stress scores vs. placebo over 6–8 weeks
  2. 02 Cortisol normalization — KSM-66 extract (600 mg/day) demonstrated a 27.9% reduction in serum cortisol in a placebo-controlled trial of stressed adults
  3. 03 Thyroid support — A randomized placebo-controlled trial found significant reductions in TSH and increases in fT3 and fT4 at 600 mg/day; monitored use recommended in pre-existing thyroid conditions

Chandrasekhar K et al. Indian J Psychological Medicine, 2012. PMC3573577. KSM-66 cortisol/stress trial, n=64. Verma N et al. Phytotherapy Research, 2025. 12-month safety confirmation, no adverse organ effects.

Rhodiola Rosea

Rhodiola rosea

Rhodiola's clinical use case is precise: cognitive performance under sustained load. Night-shift workers. High-output executives. People running on a sleep deficit and needing their prefrontal cortex to stay online. Salidroside and rosavins inhibit MAO-A and MAO-B — prolonging dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine availability. A controlled trial in 56 physicians showed statistically significant improvement in mental performance during night duty. Another in 161 cadets showed pronounced antifatigue effects from a single standardized dose. It's not a stimulant. It's a buffer against depletion.

What the trials showed
  1. 01 Mental fatigue reduction — Double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in 56 physicians showed statistically significant improvement in mental performance (Fatigue Index) during night duty
  2. 02 Cognitive performance under stress — RCT of 161 cadets demonstrated a pronounced antifatigue effect (AFI score) after a single dose of standardized SHR-5 vs. placebo
  3. 03 Burnout and stress-induced exhaustion — Multiple clinical trials support reduced burnout scores in individuals with stress-related fatigue syndrome; favorable safety profile at 200–600 mg/day

Darbinyan V et al. Phytomedicine, 2000;7(5):365–371. Shevtsov VA et al. Phytomedicine, 2003;10(2–3):95–105. Two foundational SHR-5 standardized extract RCTs establishing anti-fatigue evidence.

Berberine

Berberis aristata / Coptis chinensis

Berberine has more human trial data than most pharmaceutical cardiometabolic agents that never made it to market. An umbrella meta-analysis of 37 RCTs showed significant reductions in fasting glucose, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, LDL, triglycerides, and blood pressure. AMPK activation is the primary driver — it increases muscle glucose disposal, suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis, and shifts oxidation toward lipids. Fasting insulin dropped 28.1% in controlled studies. HOMA-IR by 44.7%. No hypoglycemia risk — the effect is conditional on hyperglycemia being present. We use it when the metabolic markers justify it.

What the trials showed
  1. 01 Blood glucose reduction — Meta-analysis of 37 RCTs (n=3,048) found berberine significantly reduced fasting plasma glucose (WMD: −0.82 mmol/L), HbA1c (WMD: −0.63%), and 2-hour postprandial glucose
  2. 02 Insulin resistance improvement — Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR reduced by 28.1% and 44.7% respectively (p<0.001) in clinical studies; no hypoglycemia risk as effect is conditional on hyperglycemia
  3. 03 Dyslipidemia and metabolic syndrome — Meta-analyses confirm improvements in LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and blood pressure across 12+ randomized placebo-controlled trials

Liang Y et al. Clinical Therapeutics, 2023. PMID: 38016844. Umbrella meta-analysis. Yin J et al. Metabolism, 2008. PMC2410097. Foundational 3-month RCT establishing berberine comparable to metformin for glycemic endpoints.

Lion's Mane

Hericium erinaceus

Lion’s Mane is the only botanical with human trial data showing actual cognitive improvement in mild cognitive impairment — not just subjective reports, but measured test performance. In a 16-week RCT in 30 MCI patients, cognitive scores improved significantly versus placebo — and reversed after discontinuation, which is exactly what you’d expect from a mechanism-based effect. Hericenones and erinacines stimulate NGF and BDNF synthesis. Erinacines cross the blood–brain barrier. A 2023 double-blind RCT in healthy adults showed faster Stroop task performance within 60 minutes of dosing. The evidence is still building — but it’s real evidence.

What the trials showed
  1. 01 Mild cognitive impairment — 16-week randomized placebo-controlled trial in 30 individuals with MCI demonstrated significant improvement in cognitive test performance vs. placebo (improvement reversed after discontinuation)
  2. 02 Cognitive processing speed — 2023 double-blind pilot RCT (n=41, healthy adults aged 18–45) showed significantly faster Stroop task performance at 60 minutes post-dose; 28-day use showed trend toward reduced stress
  3. 03 Neuroprotection and neuroinflammation — Animal studies show reduced dopaminergic cell loss in Parkinson's models and prevention of age-related cognitive decline with 8-month supplementation in aged mice

Mori K et al. Biomedical Research, 2019. PMID: 31413233. 16-week RCT in MCI patients. Docherty S et al. Nutrients, 2023;15(22):4842. PMC10675414. Double-blind pilot RCT, healthy adults.

Our Standards

Quality Standards We Require

The supplement industry is largely unregulated. We apply three non-negotiable criteria to every compound we recommend — because the difference in what your body actually receives is material.

Third-Party Testing (NSF, USP, Informed Sport)

Dietary supplements are not FDA-approved before reaching the market, meaning manufacturer claims are self-reported and unverified unless independently tested. Third-party certification programs — NSF Certified for Sport, USP Verified, and Informed Sport — test finished products for label accuracy, contaminant absence, and freedom from 270+ substances banned by major sports anti-doping agencies. Certified products undergo batch-level testing, not just formulation approval, providing assurance that what is on the label is in the bottle at the stated dose.

Bioavailable Forms

Not all nutrient forms are equally usable by the body. Synthetic forms — such as folic acid (B9), cyanocobalamin (B12), and magnesium oxide — require enzymatic conversion steps that are impaired in significant portions of the population due to genetic variants like MTHFR, or are poorly absorbed compared to active alternatives. Practitioner-grade protocols prioritize bioavailable forms: methylfolate (5-MTHF) over folic acid, methylcobalamin over cyanocobalamin, magnesium glycinate or citrate over oxide, and ubiquinol CoQ10 over ubiquinone. This distinction determines whether a supplement actually reaches target tissues in functional concentrations.

Practitioner-Grade vs. Retail

Retail supplements sold through mass-market channels are formulated to price points, not clinical outcomes — often using lower-cost synthetic forms, inadequate doses, and minimal quality oversight beyond GMP labeling requirements. Practitioner-grade supplements are produced by manufacturers that supply licensed clinicians, are subject to stricter quality controls, use clinically validated doses drawn from peer-reviewed research, and typically undergo third-party verification. The difference is not branding — it is a material distinction in what your body actually receives and can use.
