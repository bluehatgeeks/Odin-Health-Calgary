Random Supplementation
  • Copy a trend stack from social or a friend
  • Guess dose—label, double it, or whatever is loudest
  • Mix products nobody checked for overlap
  • Start it all the same week, no phases
  • Ignore food and stress; expect pills to carry everything
  • Quit months later sure that “supplements don’t work”

Result: clutter, bad interactions, and blame on pills—not the design.

Structured Supplementation
  • Intake: history, diet, sleep, stress, meds, what failed before

  • Combinations checked so pathways are not at war

Result: dose, sequence, food, and botanicals aligned to you—not a template.

  • New layer only with a reason, duration, and how you’ll judge it
Our Standards

Quality Standards We Require

Bad dose and stacking hurt—but so does a product that is not what the label claims. The category is loosely regulated; we hold three lines on anything we suggest.

Third-party verification

Not FDA-approved before sale—claims are often self-reported. NSF, USP, and Informed Sport check label against bottle, contaminants, and banned substances where relevant, at batch level—not just paperwork.

Bioavailable Forms

Many forms need conversion you may not run well; others absorb poorly. We pick active, tolerable forms tied to your context—not whatever is cheapest on the rack.

Practitioner-Grade vs. Retail

Retail lines often optimize price over outcome—weak forms, light doses, thin oversight. Practitioner channels face tighter QC, research-leaning doses, and more verification. That is uptake in the body, not packaging.

Evidence-informed practice

Literature shapes the toolbox—not your shopping impulse

Research and tradition inform the menu; your history, diet, symptoms, and tolerance pick what belongs now—not the reverse.
