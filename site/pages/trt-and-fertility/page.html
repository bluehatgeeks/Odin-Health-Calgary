Does testosterone therapy affect fertility, and what are the options?

Short answer

It often does, and for many men the effect eases after stopping. Testosterone from outside tells your brain to turn down LH and FSH, the signals your testes use to make their own testosterone and sperm, so sperm counts can fall very low while you’re on it. If children are in your plans, there are ways to raise your own testosterone while keeping production going, such as hCG and clomiphene. We help you map them against your labs and timeline.

What testosterone therapy does to sperm production

Your pituitary gland sends two signals to the testes: LH, which drives testosterone production, and FSH, which supports sperm production. When testosterone comes from outside, your brain senses plenty and turns both signals down. With less LH, the testes make less of their own testosterone, including the high level inside the testes that sperm production depends on. The AUA and Canadian Urological Association guidelines both describe this, and the Canadian guideline notes that it is often overlooked.

  • LH turns down

    The signal that tells the testes to make testosterone fades, so your own production slows.

  • FSH turns down

    The signal that supports sperm production fades too.

  • Testosterone inside the testes falls

    Healthy sperm production needs a high local testosterone level, which depends on your own LH signal.

  • Sperm counts can drop

    Counts can fall very low while you’re on treatment, and in some men to none. It varies from man to man.

Does sperm production come back after stopping?

For many men it does, but the timing is hard to predict. In male contraceptive trials of testosterone, about two-thirds of men had sperm back in the ejaculate within six months of stopping, and roughly one in ten took until the second year (AUA guideline). In a study of men who stopped therapy and used hCG and/or a SERM, older age and longer treatment predicted a lower chance of recovery at six and twelve months.

  • Months, not days

    Sperm take time to rebuild once your own signals return, so a semen analysis too early can look low.

  • Length of treatment matters

    The longer testosterone has been used, and the older you are, the slower the return tends to be.

  • Stop before you try

    The AUA advises that testosterone stops before any effort to conceive begins.

  • Not the same for everyone

    Some men, especially those who already had fertility problems, may not recover fully. A baseline tells you where you start.

Semen analysis and baseline labs before you start

Blood testosterone doesn’t show how many sperm you’re making. A semen analysis does. The AUA guideline recommends a reproductive health evaluation before treatment for men with low testosterone who are interested in fertility: a testicular exam, FSH and, where indicated, a semen analysis.

  • Semen analysis

    A baseline for sperm count, movement and shape. Counts vary from sample to sample, so a repeat can be useful. Follow the lab’s collection instructions.

  • LH and FSH

    Show whether the signal from the brain is active. High FSH alongside low testosterone typically points to impaired sperm production.

  • Testosterone, prolactin, estradiol

    Put the picture together. See our guide to the labs to ask for.

  • A safety net

    If you want a backup, ask about freezing a sample before starting anything.

Options that keep your own production going

If fertility matters, the aim is to raise your own testosterone or protect sperm production rather than replace testosterone from outside. The AUA lists aromatase inhibitors, hCG, SERMs or a combination as options for men with low testosterone who want to maintain fertility, and the Canadian guideline lists the same classes as off-label alternatives.

  • hCG

    A prescription injection that acts like LH, prompting the testes to make testosterone and keep sperm production going. Of these medicines, only hCG has FDA approval for use in males, per the AUA guideline.

  • Clomiphene and enclomiphene

    Tablets from the SERM family. They block estrogen’s brake on the pituitary so LH and FSH rise and your testes make more testosterone. Clomiphene is used off-label in men. Enclomiphene is its single-isomer form.

  • Aromatase inhibitors

    Reduce the conversion of testosterone into estrogen, which can lift your own LH and FSH. Used in selected men, off-label.

  • hCG alongside testosterone

    In one small study, low-dose hCG given with testosterone preserved sperm production in selected men. The evidence is limited.

  • Fix the causes first

    Excess weight, short sleep, heavy alcohol and some medications can lower testosterone. Weight loss and better sleep can lift it. See TRT or fix the causes first.

  • Restarting after TRT

    After stopping testosterone, men commonly use hCG and/or a SERM to restart production. Age and length of past treatment affect how quickly sperm return.

Canada and the USA: what’s available

The same medicines are discussed on both sides of the border, and rules and stock differ and change. Here is where things stand for the three most-discussed options.

  • Canada

    Health Canada lists chorionic gonadotropin products such as Pregnyl as prescription-only, with a labelled use in selected cases of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism in males. Clomiphene is authorized for women, and its use in men is off-label. Health Canada’s Drug Product Database has no enclomiphene listing.

  • USA

    FDA-approved hCG brands such as Novarel and Pregnyl are on the market. hCG has been regulated as a biologic since March 23, 2020, which ended pharmacy-compounding exemptions for it. Clomiphene is approved for women and used off-label in men. Enclomiphene has not been approved by the FDA, and where it is offered, it is through compounding.

  • On both sides

    Labeling, rules and supply keep changing. As part of your plan, we check what is available to you in your province or state.

Frequently asked questions

Does testosterone therapy cause infertility?

It can lower fertility while you are on it. Testosterone from outside tells your brain to turn down LH and FSH, the signals your testes need to make sperm, and sperm counts can fall very low, in some men to none. The American Urological Association and Canadian Urological Association guidelines both describe this effect. For many men it eases after stopping, but how quickly varies from man to man. If children are part of your plans, we help you look at options that keep your own production going.

How long does it take for sperm to come back after stopping?

It varies. In male contraceptive trials of testosterone, about two-thirds of men had sperm back in the ejaculate within six months of stopping, and roughly one in ten took until the second year (AUA guideline). In one study of men who stopped therapy and used hCG and/or a SERM, being older and having been on therapy longer were linked to a lower chance of recovery at six and twelve months. Some men do not return to their earlier level. A semen analysis after stopping shows where you stand, and we track it with you.

Can I use hCG, clomiphene or similar options instead of testosterone?

For men with low testosterone who want to keep their fertility, the AUA says aromatase inhibitors, hCG, SERMs such as clomiphene, or a combination may be used, and the Canadian guideline lists the same classes as off-label alternatives. They work by raising your own testosterone rather than replacing it. Each has its own trade-offs, response differs from man to man, and they are prescription medicines. We help you compare them against your labs and your timeline.

Are hCG and enclomiphene available in Canada and the USA?

hCG products are authorized in both countries: Health Canada lists chorionic gonadotropin products such as Pregnyl, and FDA-approved brands include Novarel and Pregnyl. In the USA, hCG has been regulated as a biologic since March 23, 2020, which ended the pharmacy-compounding exemptions for it. Clomiphene is authorized for women in both countries and used off-label in men. Enclomiphene is not an FDA-approved product, and Health Canada’s Drug Product Database has no enclomiphene listing. Rules and stock change, so we check what is available to you where you live.

Do I need a semen analysis before starting treatment?

If you may want children, it is a sensible baseline. The AUA guideline recommends a reproductive health evaluation before treatment for men with low testosterone who are interested in fertility, including a testicular exam, FSH and a semen analysis where indicated. A baseline also makes later results easier to read. We help you arrange the right tests and go through them with you. See our lab guide.
