## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 2026)
- AUA Testosterone Deficiency guideline (J Urol 2018;200:423-432, validity confirmed 2024): reproductive evaluation before treatment (exam, FSH, semen analysis where indicated); discuss long-term impact on spermatogenesis (strong rec); aromatase inhibitors, hCG, SERMs or combination may be used to maintain fertility; only hCG is FDA-approved for males; stop testosterone before conception attempts; two-thirds of men in contraceptive studies had sperm in ejaculate within 6 months, 10% not until second year; single 26-patient study of low-dose hCG + testosterone preserved spermatogenesis.
- AUA/ASRM Male Infertility guideline 2024 statement 42: for the male interested in current or future fertility, clinicians should not prescribe exogenous testosterone.
- Endocrine Society 2018: recommends against starting TRT in men planning fertility in the near term.
- CUA guideline (Can Urol Assoc J 2021;15:E234): TRT can act as a contraceptive and depress sperm production; hCG, SERMs, aromatase inhibitors are off-label alternatives; weight loss/sleep raise T.
- Kohn et al., Fertil Steril 2017;107:351: age and duration of TRT predict recovery at 6 and 12 months after hCG/SERM.
- FDA Notice to Compounders: hCG moved to biologic status March 23, 2020 (no 503A/503B exemptions). hCG labels (Novarel US, Pregnyl Canadian monograph) list "selected cases of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism in males" (checked the label PDFs). Health Canada DPD API: clomiphene citrate and chorionic gonadotropin listed, enclomiphene returns nothing (queried Oct 15 2026). Enclomiphene not FDA-approved (PMC review of NDA history, FDA PCAC 2022 compounding discussion).
- No numbers/percentages are shown as claims except the AUA "two-thirds / roughly one in ten" contraceptive-trial statement, attributed in text.
