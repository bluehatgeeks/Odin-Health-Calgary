Why shift work wears down energy, sleep and drive

Your body runs on a daily rhythm that is set mostly by light and sleep. Night and rotating shifts keep moving the target, so sleep, hunger and alertness can fall out of step with the hours you work. Shift work doesn’t lower testosterone in every man, but it stacks up several things that can.

  • {{CI:sleep:0}}

    Shorter, lighter sleep

    Daytime sleep is often shorter and more broken than night sleep, with daylight, noise and family life in the way.

  • {{CI:sun:1}}

    A clock that keeps moving

    Every switch between days and nights asks your body clock to reset. Rotations often move faster than it can follow.

  • {{CI:pulse:2}}

    Testosterone follows sleep

    In men, the daily testosterone pattern is tied closely to sleep. Short or broken sleep, especially losing the later part of it, can pull morning levels down.

  • {{CI:cup:3}}

    Load that stacks up

    Long blocks, caffeine to stay up, a drink or a screen to come down, late meals and skipped training all add to the strain.

What men on shifts tend to notice

Most men we speak with recognise a cluster rather than one symptom. Many of these overlap with low testosterone and andropause, and with plain sleep debt. Working out which is which is the first thing we do together.

  • {{CI:batt:0}}

    Energy

    Crashing after shifts, running on caffeine, wired at bedtime and flat when you wake.

  • {{CI:sleep:1}}

    Sleep

    Trouble falling asleep after nights, waking early in the daytime, never feeling fully rested.

  • {{CI:flame:2}}

    Drive and mood

    Less interest in sex, a shorter fuse, less motivation for training or time with family.

  • {{CI:body:3}}

    Body

    More weight around the middle, slower recovery from training, harder to hold on to muscle.

What we work on together, online

Generic advice like “get eight hours and keep a regular bedtime” doesn’t survive a rotation. We plan your full 24 hours around the shifts you work, then adjust it as you go.

  • {{CI:sleep:0}}

    An anchor sleep window

    A core block of sleep you protect on every rotation, so your body has one fixed point when shifts move. Naps are planned, not accidental.

  • {{CI:sun:1}}

    Light, used on purpose

    Bright light when your shift starts, and less light on the way home and before sleep, can help your body clock follow your schedule.

  • {{CI:cup:2}}

    Caffeine and alcohol timing

    Caffeine early in the shift instead of late, and a wind-down routine that doesn’t depend on a drink.

  • {{CI:food:3}}

    Meals that fit the shift

    Main meals around your waking hours, lighter food in the small hours, and a simple plan for what you eat at work.

  • {{CI:train:4}}

    Training around rotations

    Strength work and movement booked for the days and times you have energy, so recovery keeps up.

  • {{CI:calendar:5}}

    Switching between nights and days

    A routine for the first night back and the first day off, so each changeover costs you less.

Getting testosterone tested on a shift schedule

Testosterone is usually measured in the morning because levels tend to be highest after sleep. On shifts, “morning” means after your main sleep, whatever the clock says. Timing the test well makes the result easier to read.

  • {{CI:clock:0}}

    Soon after you wake

    Canadian guidelines recommend a sample within about three hours of waking for men who work shifts, ideally fasting.

  • {{CI:calendar:1}}

    On a steady stretch

    Book it when your sleep pattern has been stable, not the day after switching between nights and days.

  • {{CI:tube:2}}

    Same lab, and a repeat

    Results vary from day to day and between labs, so repeat tests at the same lab. A single low result is usually repeated before anything is decided.

Sources: BC Guidelines, Testosterone Testing protocol · Morales et al., Canadian clinical practice guideline on testosterone deficiency, CMAJ
