3. **Health statements on the page, with their basis:**
   - "Shift work doesn't lower testosterone in every man, but it stacks up several things that can" and "short or broken sleep, especially losing the later part of it, can pull morning levels down": based on "Sleep timing may modulate the effect of sleep loss on testosterone", Clin Endocrinol 2012 (PubMed 22568763, abstract read via search; the PubMed page blocks the fetch tool: sleep loss in the second half of the night lowered morning testosterone; early-night loss did not) and "Standardising the biochemical confirmation of adult male hypogonadism", Clin Endocrinol (doi 10.1111/cen.14929), noting the testosterone day pattern is driven mainly by sleep, not the clock. Evidence on shift work itself is mixed (some studies show delayed timing, not lower totals). Not cited on the page; wording kept cautious.
   - "Daytime sleep is often shorter and more broken", "light ... can help your body clock follow your schedule", caffeine early in the shift, lighter food in the small hours, anchor sleep: general shift-work sleep hygiene, unsourced on the page. Add citations or clinician sign-off if needed.
   - Testing section: "within about three hours of waking for men who work shifts" (CMAJ 2015 guideline, rec. 3), "preferably fasting", "stable sleep pattern, not during shift changes", "same lab for repeat tests" (BC Guidelines), "a single low result is usually repeated" (BC Guidelines biological variation note; Clin Endocrinol doi 10.1111/cen.14929). Both primary sources linked on the page and were fetched live on 2026-10-15. These are Canadian sources; the page serves the USA too (the Endocrine Society guideline gives similar morning-sample advice, not linked).
