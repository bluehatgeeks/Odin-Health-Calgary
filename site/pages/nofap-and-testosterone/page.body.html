Does NoFap raise testosterone? What the day 7 study really showed

Short answer

No study that still stands shows that NoFap raises testosterone in a lasting way. The popular claim that testosterone peaks on the seventh day without ejaculating comes from one study of 28 men, published in 2003 and retracted by the journal on December 10, 2021, because it overlapped an earlier paper by the same lead author. Even in that study, levels changed very little on days two to five and did not keep rising after the peak. On the other side, a study of 31,925 US men linked frequent ejaculation, 21 or more times a month, with about a 20 percent lower risk of prostate cancer than 4 to 7 times. NoFap and testosterone at a glance

NoFap means going without masturbation, and often without ejaculating at all, for days or months. One of the most shared reasons for trying it is the idea that it raises testosterone. Here is what the research behind that idea says, and what a much larger study found about ejaculation and the prostate.

Where the day 7 claim comes from

The number most NoFap posts quote traces back to a single paper: Jiang and colleagues, “A research on the relationship between ejaculation and serum testosterone level in men,” published in the Journal of Zhejiang University Science in 2003. The researchers measured serum testosterone every day in 28 volunteers during two periods of abstinence after ejaculating. They reported that testosterone changed very little from the second to the fifth day, then showed a clear peak on the seventh day, at 145.7 percent of baseline. After that peak, they saw no regular change with longer abstinence. In other words, even the study behind the claim did not find that a longer streak meant more testosterone.

Why the study was retracted

On December 10, 2021, the journal published a retraction note. The Editor-in-Chief retracted the 2003 paper because it significantly overlaps an article published earlier in Chinese by the lead author: “Periodic changes in serum testosterone levels after ejaculation in men,” in Acta Physiologica Sinica in 2002. Two of the four authors agreed to the retraction, and the editor could not reach the other two. The note gives overlap as the only reason and does not say the data were made up. Still, a retracted paper is taken out of the reliable record, so the day 7 peak rests on one small study that the journal itself has withdrawn.

Ejaculation frequency and the prostate

The larger body of evidence on ejaculation points the other way from NoFap. In the Health Professionals Follow-up Study, 31,925 US men reported how often they ejaculated each month at ages 20 to 29 and 40 to 49, and were followed from 1992 to 2010 (Rider and colleagues, European Urology, 2016). Men who reported 21 or more ejaculations a month had about a 20 percent lower risk of prostate cancer than men who reported 4 to 7: a hazard ratio of 0.81 for frequency in their twenties and 0.78 in their forties. The link was driven by lower-risk cancers, and it held when the researchers looked only at men who had PSA screening. This is an observational study, so it shows a link, not proof that ejaculating more prevents prostate cancer. It does mean there is no research reason to see regular ejaculation as harmful to your hormones or your prostate.
