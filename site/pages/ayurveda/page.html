Ayurveda in plain language: your constitution

Ayurveda describes three doshas, Vata, Pitta and Kapha, as a way of naming patterns in how people run. Everyone has all three, in a different mix. That mix is your constitution, called Prakriti. How you feel right now, your Vikriti, can drift away from it with stress, short sleep, travel, shift work or a change of season. Your plan uses both, so the advice fits you instead of a one-size-fits-all routine.

  • Vata: movement

    Described as quick, light and creative. Out of rhythm, it tends to show up as restless sleep, scattered energy and an irregular appetite.

  • Pitta: heat and drive

    Described as focused, competitive and warm. Out of rhythm, it tends to show up as irritability, running hot and pushing too hard for too long.

  • Kapha: steadiness

    Described as strong, calm and steady. Out of rhythm, it tends to show up as slow mornings, a heavy feeling and low motivation.

Four pillars of your Ayurvedic plan

Each pillar is chosen for your constitution and your current state, and you start with the one or two changes most likely to matter for you.

  • Food for your constitution

    Meals and foods chosen for your body type, how your digestion is doing and the season: warm and grounding for some, lighter and cooler for others. Regular meal times, no crash diet.

  • Daily rhythm

    Ayurveda’s daily routine, dinacharya: steady times to wake, eat, move, wind down and sleep. We adapt it for long hours, travel and shift work.

  • Herbs, where they fit

    Traditional Ayurvedic herbs suggested only where they suit you, from suppliers that test for quality, and checked against your medications first.

  • Breath and movement

    Breathing practice (pranayama), yoga-based mobility, walking and strength work, matched to your energy rather than an all-or-nothing programme.

A day that works with your rhythm

Ayurveda pays close attention to timing. In its tradition, digestion is strongest around midday and the evening is for slowing down. Your plan turns that into a simple daily shape you can follow, and we adjust it to your work, family and training.

What an Ayurvedic day might look like

  • Wake at a steady time and get some daylight early.
  • A warm, simple breakfast that suits your digestion.
  • Your main meal around midday.
  • Movement when your energy is highest.
  • A lighter, earlier dinner.
  • A wind-down routine and a regular bedtime.

Energy, sleep & habits

Can I raise testosterone naturally?

The best evidence is for losing extra body fat, sleeping enough and staying active. Most “booster” ingredients have little human evidence.

How does sleep affect testosterone?

Testosterone rises while you sleep, so short, broken or badly timed sleep can pull it down.

Is it aging, or weight, sleep and stress?

Usually a mix. Age brings a gradual decline, and extra weight, short sleep and stress can pull testosterone lower. Those are the parts you can work on.

Divide ng/dL by 28.84 to get nmol/L. The calculator also estimates free T from total T, SHBG and albumin.

What are the side effects of TRT?

The most common is a rise in red blood cells (hematocrit). Acne, a small PSA rise and lower sperm production are also linked, and most show up early on labs.

Does TRT affect fertility?

It often lowers sperm production while you’re on it, and for many men that eases after stopping. If children are in your plans, we help you weigh the options.

Does testosterone cause hair loss?

Pattern hair loss comes from DHT acting on scalp follicles your genes make sensitive, not from your blood testosterone level.

Frequently asked questions

What is Ayurveda, and what is a dosha?

Ayurveda is a traditional system of health from India. It describes three doshas, Vata, Pitta and Kapha, as a way of naming patterns in how a person runs: movement and change, heat and drive, steadiness and structure. Everyone has all three in their own mix. That mix is your constitution, and it guides the food, daily rhythm, herbs and movement in your plan.

Can Ayurveda help men with low-T symptoms like low energy and poor sleep?

Ayurveda works on the daily habits that shape how you feel: sleep, stress, food, movement and routine. Men with low testosterone symptoms often describe low energy, poor sleep and low drive, and these habits are part of that picture. We build a plan around your constitution and adjust it at every check-in. To find out whether low testosterone is part of your picture, see which blood tests to ask for.

Which herbs might be in my plan?

Herbs are one option among four pillars, and we suggest them only where they fit you. Ashwagandha, for example, is a traditional Ayurvedic herb that many men ask about. We point you to products from suppliers that test for quality and purity, because some imported Ayurvedic products have been found to contain heavy metals. In Canada, licensed natural health products carry an eight-digit NPN on the label. See our supplements and herbology page for how we build a plan.

How does Ayurveda work with my doctor and my medications?

Ayurveda works alongside your medical care. Some herbs, including ashwagandha, can interact with medications such as those for thyroid, blood pressure or diabetes, and with sedatives, so we check your medication list before suggesting any herb and ask you to confirm with your doctor or pharmacist. We can share your plan with your doctor if you choose, and we factor in anything they change.
