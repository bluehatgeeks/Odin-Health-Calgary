Ayurveda in plain language: your constitution

Ayurveda describes three doshas, Vata, Pitta and Kapha, as a way of naming patterns in how people run. Everyone has all three, in a different mix. That mix is your constitution, called Prakriti. How you feel right now, your Vikriti, can drift away from it with stress, short sleep, travel, shift work or a change of season. Your plan uses both, so the advice fits you instead of a one-size-fits-all routine.

  • {{CI:wind:0}}

    Vata: movement

    Described as quick, light and creative. Out of rhythm, it tends to show up as restless sleep, scattered energy and an irregular appetite.

  • {{CI:flame:1}}

    Pitta: heat and drive

    Described as focused, competitive and warm. Out of rhythm, it tends to show up as irritability, running hot and pushing too hard for too long.

  • {{CI:drop:2}}

    Kapha: steadiness

    Described as strong, calm and steady. Out of rhythm, it tends to show up as slow mornings, a heavy feeling and low motivation.

Four pillars of your Ayurvedic plan

Each pillar is chosen for your constitution and your current state, and you start with the one or two changes most likely to matter for you.

  • {{CI:food:0}}

    Food for your constitution

    Meals and foods chosen for your body type, how your digestion is doing and the season: warm and grounding for some, lighter and cooler for others. Regular meal times, no crash diet.

  • {{CI:clock:1}}

    Daily rhythm

    Ayurveda’s daily routine, dinacharya: steady times to wake, eat, move, wind down and sleep. We adapt it for long hours, travel and shift work.

  • {{CI:leaf:2}}

    Herbs, where they fit

    Traditional Ayurvedic herbs suggested only where they suit you, from suppliers that test for quality, and checked against your medications first.

  • {{CI:breath:3}}

    Breath and movement

    Breathing practice (pranayama), yoga-based mobility, walking and strength work, matched to your energy rather than an all-or-nothing programme.

A day that works with your rhythm

Ayurveda pays close attention to timing. In its tradition, digestion is strongest around midday and the evening is for slowing down. Your plan turns that into a simple daily shape you can follow, and we adjust it to your work, family and training.

What an Ayurvedic day might look like

  • Wake at a steady time and get some daylight early.
  • A warm, simple breakfast that suits your digestion.
  • Your main meal around midday.
  • Movement when your energy is highest.
  • A lighter, earlier dinner.
  • A wind-down routine and a regular bedtime.
