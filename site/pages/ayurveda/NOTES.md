## Copy / compliance
Removed from the old live page: "We Do Not Practice Medicine. We Practice Health.", all Calgary wording ("Odin Lab Calgary"), "5,000 Years of Personalised Medicine" / "5,000+ years of clinical tradition", "Root cause, not symptom", the "Ancient framework, modern evidence" block (incl. "Ashwagandha reduces cortisol by 27.9%", VO2 max, Triphala "detoxification"), the research list, "Body Reset / Guided Detox Protocol", "Guided Detox Program" (herbal oils, steam, "clear built-up waste", "resetting your metabolism"), "12 Conditions Addressed" (chronic fatigue, autoimmune, thyroid, hormonal imbalance, etc.), "restore function", "fix the source", "Book an Ayurvedic Assessment" as a clinical assessment.

What the page says instead: what Odin does (online Ayurvedic consultation, constitution + current state, written plan across food / daily rhythm / herbs where they fit / breath and movement, check-ins), framed as habits and routine. Dosha descriptions are given as traditional descriptions ("Described as ..."). The only timing statement ("in its tradition, digestion is strongest around midday") is attributed to Ayurvedic tradition. No outcome, efficacy, disease, hormone or testosterone claims; low-T is handled by linking to the labs and TRT answer pages. No disclaimer paragraph, no 911/988 block, no "Illustration only" captions, no "physician-aware" / "not a treatment" wording. The words doctor/pharmacist appear only in the FAQ "How does Ayurveda work with my doctor and my medications?" (build.py asserts this, plus no detox/cleanse/5,000/cortisol wording outside the FAQ).

Facts used in the FAQ, verified 2026-10-15:
- Ashwagandha: traditional Ayurvedic herb; "might interact with some medications, including those for diabetes and high blood pressure, ... sedatives, ... thyroid hormone medications" - NCCIH, https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ashwagandha (last updated March 2023). No ashwagandha benefit claim is made on the page.
- "Some Ayurvedic preparations may contain lead, mercury, or arsenic" / FDA warning about metals - NCCIH, https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/ayurvedic-medicine-in-depth. Page wording: "some imported Ayurvedic products have been found to contain heavy metals".
- Canada: licensed natural health products carry an eight-digit NPN (Health Canada; general knowledge, same claim as on /supplements).

Flags for a human:
1. **Who delivers Ayurveda** (title, training, credentials) isn't stated; Ayurveda practice is not licensed in most of Canada/USA. The page says "we" / "your Ayurvedic consultation". Confirm who runs the sessions and whether they may suggest herbs in every province/state served.
2. **Does Odin sell herbs?** Copy only says "we point you to products from suppliers that test for quality"; no products, prices or affiliate claims. Add disclosures if Odin sells or earns commission.
3. **Meta description** ("See how it can support energy, sleep and stress") is the owner-approved SEO text; on-page copy is more cautious (habits, structure, no outcome promise).
4. The shared nav/footer (loaded from GitHub) may still carry Calgary wording; the page body has none.
