## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 16 2026)
- Endocrine Society 2018 (Bhasin et al., JCEM 2018;103:1715, doi 10.1210/jc.2018-00229; PubMed/abstract + hosted PDF): diagnose hypogonadism only with symptoms/signs + unequivocally and consistently low T; fasting morning total T as initial test, confirm by repeating; free T when total near lower limit or SHBG-altering condition; look for the cause. About 30% of men with an initial T in the hypogonadal range have a normal T on repeat (page: "about three in ten"). T affected by acute illness, nutritional deficiency, opioids, glucocorticoids, age, obesity, diabetes, OSA; morning peak; food/glucose suppress T.
- AUA 2018 testosterone deficiency guideline (Mulhall et al.; auanet.org; validity confirmed 2024): TD = low T plus symptoms/signs; symptoms "very non-specific" (chronic fatigue, chronic stress, depressed state). Total T <300 ng/dL on two early-morning measurements.
- CUA 2021 (Grober et al., CUAJ 2021;15(5):E234-43, doi 10.5489/cuaj.7252): morning total T 7–11 am; <10 nmol/L reasonable threshold; some men symptomatic at higher levels; calculated free/bioavailable T for equivocal results; LH to classify primary vs secondary; potentially correctable causes first (acute illness, obesity, opioids/glucocorticoids, extreme nutritional deficiency, extreme exercise, sleep apnea, hyperprolactinemia, hemochromatosis, pituitary disorders); rule out overlapping conditions (depression, hypothyroidism, sleep disorders).
- EMAS, Wu et al., NEJM 2010;363:123-35 (doi 10.1056/NEJMoa0911101): 3,369 men aged 40–79; three sexual symptoms had the clearest syndromic link with low T.
- NHS "The 'male menopause'" (nhs.uk, reviewed 13 Oct  midlife symptoms often from stress, sleep, alcohol, weight or mood rather than hormones; late-onset hypogonadism from symptoms + blood tests.
