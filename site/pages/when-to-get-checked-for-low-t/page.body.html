When should you get checked for low testosterone?

Short answer

Testing is most useful when sexual symptoms show up with fatigue, body changes or mood shifts — not from vague tiredness alone. Low testosterone is confirmed when those symptoms line up with two fasting, early-morning total testosterone tests. Illness, opioids, short sleep and weight can temporarily lower levels, so those get checked alongside the labs. Symptom clusters that make testing useful

The clearest cue is a cluster, not a single vague complaint. In the European Male Ageing Study (Wu and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, 2010), researchers surveyed 3,369 men aged 40 to 79 and found that the three sexual symptoms — poor morning erections, low sexual desire and erectile dysfunction — had the strongest link with lower testosterone. Fatigue, low mood and less capacity for vigorous activity were related too, but less specifically. The Endocrine Society (2018) groups the more suggestive signs as sexual (reduced desire and spontaneous erections), physical (less energy and muscle, more body fat, lower bone density) and mood or thinking (low mood, poor concentration, sleep problems). The Canadian Urological Association (2021) uses a similar three-way list. Isolated tiredness is common and usually has other drivers; see the full picture in low testosterone symptoms in men.

Why two early-morning fasting tests

Low testosterone is not diagnosed from one blood draw. The Endocrine Society recommends measuring fasting morning total testosterone with an accurate assay, then repeating it to confirm, because testosterone peaks in the morning, food and glucose suppress it, and levels swing from day to day. In that guideline, about three in ten men whose first result looked low had a normal result on repeat. The American Urological Association defines testosterone deficiency as symptoms plus a total testosterone below 300 ng/dL on two early-morning measurements. The Canadian Urological Association recommends a morning draw between 7 and 11 a.m. and suggests below 10 nmol/L as a reasonable threshold, while noting that some men have symptoms at higher levels. Timing and a second sample are what make the number trustworthy.

What can temporarily lower testosterone

A low reading does not always mean a stable problem. The Endocrine Society notes that testosterone is affected by acute illness, nutritional deficiency, opioids, glucocorticoids, age, obesity, diabetes and sleep apnea. The Canadian Urological Association lists potentially correctable causes to address first: acute illness, obesity, opioids and glucocorticoids, extreme nutritional deficiency, extreme exercise, sleep apnea, high prolactin, hemochromatosis and pituitary disorders. Short or broken sleep also pulls levels down. Checking these alongside the labs matters, because a draw during a flu, a stretch of bad nights or while taking opioids may not show your usual baseline. Weight, sleep and stress can also shift testosterone over months; see low T from aging or from weight, sleep and stress.

    Obesity and type 2 diabetes are common drivers; weight change moves testosterone with it.

    Crash dieting and overtraining are listed Canadian causes to correct before treating the number alone.

When total T is borderline: free T, SHBG and LH

Not every useful result sits clearly below the cut-off. When total testosterone is near the lower limit, or when something may change SHBG (the protein that binds testosterone in the blood), the Endocrine Society recommends checking free testosterone by equilibrium dialysis or a validated calculation. Aging, obesity and some medications shift SHBG, so free T can tell a different story than total T. LH then helps classify the pattern: high LH points toward the testes; low or low-normal LH points toward the brain’s signal, and that is when prolactin may be checked. The Canadian Urological Association likewise uses calculated free or bioavailable testosterone for equivocal totals. Full panel detail is in low testosterone labs to ask for.

Reading your results

How do I convert ng/dL to nmol/L?

Divide ng/dL by 28.84 to get nmol/L. The calculator also estimates free T from total T, SHBG and albumin.

Free vs total testosterone: what’s the difference?

Total T counts all the testosterone in your blood. Free T is the 2 to 4 percent that isn’t bound to SHBG or albumin.

Habits & treatment

Can I raise testosterone naturally?

The best evidence is for losing extra body fat, sleeping enough and staying active. Most “booster” ingredients have little human evidence.

How does sleep affect testosterone?

Testosterone rises while you sleep, so short, broken or badly timed sleep can pull it down.

What are the side effects of TRT?

The most common is a rise in red blood cells (hematocrit). Acne, a small PSA rise and lower sperm production are also linked, and most show up early on labs.

Does testosterone cause hair loss?

Pattern hair loss comes from DHT acting on scalp follicles your genes make sensitive, not from your blood testosterone level.
