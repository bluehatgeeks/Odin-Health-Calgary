## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 16 2026; PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities efetch, saved in /workspace/research-trtse/abstracts.txt)
- Endocrine Society 2018 (Bhasin S et al, JCEM 2018;103(5):1715-44, doi 10.1210/jc.2018-00229; full text /workspace/research-trtse/es2018.txt): Table 8 adverse outcomes with clear evidence of association: erythrocytosis, acne and oily skin, detection of subclinical prostate cancer, growth of metastatic prostate cancer, reduced sperm production and fertility; uncommon with weak evidence: gynecomastia, male pattern balding (familial), growth of breast cancer, induction or worsening of obstructive sleep apnea; formulation-specific: IM injections (fluctuation in mood/libido, injection pain), patches (skin reactions), gels (transfer to partner/others, skin irritation), buccal (taste alteration); erythrocytosis the most frequent adverse event in trials; commissioned meta-analysis erythrocytosis (hct >54 percent) RR 8.14 ("about eight times"); hct rise related to dose/concentration and larger in older men; baseline hct >48 percent (>50 at high altitude) a caution; Table 9 monitoring: hct at baseline, 3-6 months, then annually; if >54 percent stop, evaluate for hypoxia and sleep apnea, reinitiate at reduced dose; phlebotomy effective; evaluate at 3-12 months then annually; T at 3-6 months aiming mid-normal; cypionate/enanthate measured midway between injections, adjust if >600 or <350 ng/dL; men 55-69 (and 40-69 at increased risk) choosing monitoring: PSA + DRE before starting and at 3-12 months then standard screening; closer prostate work-up if PSA rises >1.4 ng/mL in 12 months, confirmed PSA >4, DRE abnormality or worse urinary symptoms; average PSA rise 0.3 ng/mL in young men and 0.44 in older men (systematic review cited by ES); TTrials PSA rise >1.4: 2.4 percent at 3 months and 4.7 percent at 12 months on T vs 1.6 and 0.6 on placebo; do not start with PSA >4 (>3 in higher risk, e.g. African American men or first-degree relative with prostate cancer), untreated severe OSA, or fertility planned soon (T suppresses spermatogenesis).
- CUA 2021 (Grober ED et al, CUAJ 2021;15(5):E234-43, doi 10.5489/cuaj.7252, PMC8095276; /workspace/research-trtse/cua2021.txt): polycythemia common; for hct >=55 percent: dose reduction, drug holiday, different formulation or blood donation/phlebotomy; monitoring baseline, 3 months, 6 months, yearly; exogenous T can act as a contraceptive; men seeking fertility should consider hCG, SERMs (clomiphene) or aromatase inhibitors; nasal gel may suppress spermatogenesis less; measure estradiol before therapy in men with breast symptoms/gynecomastia; T converted to estradiol (aromatization), more so in adipose tissue; sleep apnea a potentially correctable cause; meta-analysis of 22 RCTs with 2,351 men: no higher prostate cancer risk.
- TRAVERSE: Lincoff AM et al, NEJM 2023;389(2):107-117, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2215025, PMID 37326322: 5,246 men 45-80, preexisting or high risk of CV disease, hypogonadal symptoms and two fasting T <300 ng/dL; 1.62 percent T gel vs placebo gel; mean follow-up 33.0 months ("close to three years"); primary MACE (CV death, nonfatal MI, nonfatal stroke) 7.0 vs 7.3 percent, HR 0.96 (95 percent CI 0.78-1.17), noninferior; higher incidence of atrial fibrillation, acute kidney injury and pulmonary embolism with T (abstract only; no rates stated on the page).
- TRAVERSE prostate: Bhasin S et al, JAMA Netw Open 2023;6(12):e2348692, PMID 38150256: 5,204 men analyzed, PSA >3.0 and IPSS >19 excluded; high-grade prostate cancer 0.19 vs 0.12 percent (HR 1.62, P = .51, not significant); any prostate cancer, acute urinary retention, invasive procedures, biopsies and new pharmacologic treatment did not differ; PSA rose more with T.
- TRAVERSE fractures: Snyder PJ et al, NEJM 2024;390(3):203-211, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2308836, PMID 38231621: clinical fractures 3.50 vs 2.46 percent (HR 1.43).
- Calof OM et al, J Gerontol A 2005;60(11):1451-7, PMID 16339333: meta-analysis of 19 RCTs, men >=45; prostate events OR 1.78; hct >50 percent OR 3.69 ("nearly four times"); CV events, sleep apnea and deaths not significantly different.
- Fernandez-Balsells MM et al, JCEM 2010;95(6):2560-75, PMID 20525906: 51 studies; hct +3.18 percent (WMD; page "about 3 points"), Hb +0.80 g/dL, HDL -0.49 mg/dL (page "small drop in HDL").
- Ory J et al, J Urol 2022;207(6):1295-1301, PMID 35050717: multi-institutional health-records database (74 million patients); men on TT with polycythemia (hct >=52 percent) vs matched men with normal hct on TT: MACE/VTE in first year 5.15 vs 3.87 percent, OR 1.35.
- Rivero MJ et al, J Urol 2023;210(1):162-170, PMID 37126399: RCT IM cypionate vs intranasal T; hct 42.7 -> 46.6 percent after 4 months on injections, no significant change on nasal; estradiol rose on injections, not on nasal.
- Coviello AD et al, JCEM 2005;90(5):2595-602, PMID 15713727: healthy men, 200 mg TE weekly; LH and FSH suppressed to 5 and 3 percent of baseline; intratesticular T down 94 percent within 3 weeks.
- Palacios A et al, J Urol 1981;126(1):46-8, doi 10.1016/s0022-5347(17)54372-4, PMID 6788960: 39 normal men, TE 200 mg; after 4 months weekly or every-other-week injections testicular volume down 19.0 and 16.5 percent; return to normal size after treatment stopped.
- WHO Task Force, Lancet 1990;336(8721):955-9, PMID 1977002: 271 healthy men, 200 mg TE weekly; 157 azoospermic (cumulative 65 percent at 6 months); median recovery to 20 million/mL 3.7 months after stopping ("just under four months").
- FDA (fda.gov, fetched Oct 16 2026; /workspace/research-trtse/fda.txt): Feb 28 2025 class-wide labeling changes: TRAVERSE results added to all testosterone products, boxed-warning language about adverse CV outcomes removed, new warning on blood pressure increases based on ambulatory BP monitoring studies across products; Testosterone Information page: updates requested in June 2026 incl. removing the age-related limitation of use and revising prostate cancer/BPH safety information.
- AUA 2018 (Mulhall JP et al; auanet.org, reviewed 2024): a man is a candidate for testosterone therapy only when low levels come with symptoms (single short attributed fact, as on sibling pages).
- Fetched but NOT used: Crosnoe 2013 (PMID 26813847). Dropped for lack of a fetched source: DHT/5-alpha-reductase hair mechanism, any AF/AKI/PE rates, any VTE rate.
