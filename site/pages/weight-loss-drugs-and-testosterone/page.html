Does tirzepatide affect testosterone levels? What semaglutide and other GLP-1 drugs do to testosterone in men

Short answer

In studies of men with overweight or obesity, testosterone usually rises. Meta-analyses of GLP-1 drugs such as semaglutide and liraglutide, plus early tirzepatide studies, report higher testosterone alongside weight loss. The big SURMOUNT (tirzepatide) and STEP (semaglutide) weight-loss trials did not measure it. Fast weight loss also takes some muscle with it, about a quarter of the weight lost on tirzepatide in SURMOUNT-1, so the best results come from pairing the drug with labs, protein, strength training and sleep. What the research shows

Four recent systematic reviews looked at GLP-1 drugs in men. A 2026 meta-analysis in Andrology (Corona and colleagues; 8 GLP-1 studies, 375 men with overweight or obesity) found that GLP-1 drugs significantly raised total and calculated free testosterone along with LH and FSH. A 2025 Andrology meta-analysis (Salvio and colleagues; 7 studies, 680 men) found a significant rise in total testosterone, with LH, FSH, SHBG and free testosterone rising too, and larger weight loss went with a larger rise. A 2026 Journal of Sexual Medicine review (Deameh and colleagues; 10 studies, 639 men) found total testosterone consistently higher, with LH and FSH preserved or raised and free testosterone changes inconsistent. A 2025 BMC Urology meta-analysis (Orra and colleagues; 4 studies, 219 men) found bioavailable testosterone higher.

  • Higher in all four

    Total or bioavailable testosterone rose after GLP-1 treatment in each of the four reviews.

  • The brain’s signal held up

    LH and FSH were preserved or raised, while the testosterone-therapy comparison groups in the Deameh review showed suppression. See our TRT and fertility guide.

  • Free testosterone varies

    Free testosterone rose in the Corona and Salvio analyses, was unchanged in the Orra review and varied in the Deameh review, where SHBG often rose at the same time.

  • Your numbers are the answer

    Averages hide a wide range, so we compare your own labs before and during treatment.

Tirzepatide and semaglutide trials: what they found for testosterone

Does tirzepatide affect testosterone levels? In men with obesity, the studies so far suggest it raises them, mostly in step with the weight lost, although the large SURMOUNT-1 trial never measured testosterone. In a two-month controlled pilot of 83 men with obesity and low testosterone (La Vignera and colleagues, 2025), the tirzepatide group ended with higher total, free and bioavailable testosterone than men on diet and walking alone. Weight loss by itself is linked to higher testosterone: a 2013 meta-analysis of 24 studies found total testosterone rose by about 2.9 nmol/L after low-calorie diets.

The large trials behind tirzepatide and semaglutide were built to measure weight, not hormones. SURMOUNT-1 (Jastreboff and colleagues, NEJM 2022; 2,539 adults without diabetes, a 72-week trial) found average weight loss of 15.0, 19.5 and 20.9 percent on 5, 10 and 15 mg of tirzepatide, against 3.1 percent on placebo, along with better cardiometabolic markers and, in a substudy, body-composition scans. STEP 1 (Wilding and colleagues, NEJM 2021; 1,961 adults, a 68-week trial) found 14.9 percent weight loss on semaglutide 2.4 mg against 2.4 percent on placebo. Testosterone is not among the registered outcomes of either trial (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04184622 and NCT03548935), and neither paper reports it. The testosterone answers come from smaller studies in men, and those are below.

Saxenda (liraglutide) for weight management: male-specific considerations

Are there male-specific considerations when evaluating Saxenda for weight management? Yes: in men, Saxenda (liraglutide 3.0 mg daily) has been studied for its effect on testosterone, LH, FSH and sperm, and the small studies so far point up, while the large trial behind the dose enrolled mostly women. The SCALE trial (Pi-Sunyer and colleagues, NEJM 2015; 3,731 adults without type 2 diabetes, a 56-week trial) found average weight loss of 8.4 kg on liraglutide 3.0 mg against 2.8 kg on placebo, but 78.5 percent of the people in it were women and the paper does not report testosterone. In a small retrospective study of 47 people who stayed on liraglutide 3.0 mg for at least six months (16 of them men; Milani and colleagues, 2024), the men lost more weight than the women.

For hormones, the best data in men are two small studies. In a randomized 16-week trial of 30 men with obesity and low testosterone (Jensterle and colleagues, Endocrine Connections 2019; average BMI 41.2), liraglutide 3.0 mg daily raised total testosterone by 2.6 nmol/L against 5.9 on testosterone gel, but only liraglutide raised LH and FSH, the brain’s signal to the testes, and men on liraglutide lost 7.9 kg against 0.9 kg. In a four-month study of 110 men aged 18 to 35 with metabolic hypogonadism (La Vignera and colleagues, Journal of Clinical Medicine 2023), the men given liraglutide 3 mg daily ended with higher total testosterone, SHBG, LH and FSH than men given gonadotropins or testosterone gel, along with better sperm parameters and erectile function. Men in that study were grouped by whether they wanted children, not at random. Because SHBG rises as weight falls, free testosterone is worth reading alongside total; our free vs total testosterone guide explains why.

Two more points matter for men. If you are planning a family, keeping LH and FSH switched on is the difference from testosterone therapy, which lowered LH, FSH and sperm counts in the semaglutide comparison trial above; our TRT and fertility guide covers the trade-offs. In a semen substudy of the S-LITE trial (Andersen and colleagues, Human Reproduction 2022; 56 men with obesity), sperm concentration and sperm count rose after an eight-week low-calorie diet and stayed higher for a year in the men who kept the weight off, with liraglutide, exercise or both as the maintenance plan. In the main S-LITE trial, liraglutide plus exercise cut body-fat percentage by about twice as much as either alone, which is why we pair any weight-loss medication with the strength-training plan in the muscle section below.

Why it rises: weight loss, and possibly more

Weight loss is the best-established route. A 2013 meta-analysis of 24 studies (Corona and colleagues) found that total testosterone rose by about 2.9 nmol/L after low-calorie diets and about 8.7 nmol/L after bariatric surgery. A 2026 systematic review of incretin drugs (La Vignera and Condorelli) reported average total testosterone rises of 2.5 to 5.2 nmol/L with semaglutide, liraglutide and tirzepatide in men with obesity-related low testosterone, largely tied to weight loss and better insulin sensitivity. The 2026 Corona meta-analysis found the rises independent of weight loss and suggests a direct effect on the hypothalamus-pituitary-testis axis. The reviews agree on the direction, and research on the mechanism continues.

    The La Vignera review links the testosterone rise to better insulin sensitivity as well as weight loss.

  • A possible direct effect

    The 2026 Corona analysis points to the brain-to-testes axis itself, a question that new trials can settle.

Real-world numbers and a tirzepatide pilot

A retrospective chart review presented at ENDO 2025 (Portillo Canales, Dhindsa and colleagues) followed 110 men on semaglutide, dulaglutide or tirzepatide (average age 54, 86 with type 2 diabetes, about 1.6 years of therapy). Average weight fell from 116 to 104 kg, total testosterone rose from 322 to 380 ng/dL, and free testosterone from 7.0 to 8.2 ng/dL. The share of men with total testosterone above 300 ng/dL rose from 53 percent to 77 percent, and more weight lost went with a bigger rise. A controlled pilot in Reproductive Biology and Endocrinology (La Vignera and colleagues, 83 men with obesity and low testosterone, aged about 55) compared tirzepatide with lifestyle change alone and with transdermal testosterone for two months. The tirzepatide group had larger rises in LH, FSH, SHBG, total, free and bioavailable testosterone, and lower estradiol.

    The share above 300 ng/dL moved from 53 percent to 77 percent with about 10 percent weight loss.

Protecting muscle while the weight comes off

Fast weight loss takes some lean mass with the fat. In a body-composition substudy of SURMOUNT-1 (160 adults, 73 percent women, DXA scans at baseline and over a 72-week trial), tirzepatide cut body weight by 21.3 percent, fat mass by 33.9 percent and lean mass by 10.9 percent, and about 75 percent of the weight lost was fat and 25 percent was lean mass. A 2025 joint advisory from the American College of Lifestyle Medicine, the American Society for Nutrition, the Obesity Medicine Association and The Obesity Society recommends baseline muscle-strength and body-composition assessment and preserving muscle and bone through resistance training and an appropriate diet. In the S-LITE trial (195 adults, one year after a low-calorie diet), exercise plus liraglutide cut body-fat percentage by 3.9 points, about twice the drop of either alone. A 2026 review in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism (Friedl and colleagues) adds that severe energy deficit and disrupted sleep turn down the brain’s LH signal.

Reading your labs: total, free and SHBG

SHBG tends to rise as weight falls, so total and free testosterone can move by different amounts. The 2026 Journal of Sexual Medicine review found free testosterone changes inconsistent and often offset by rising SHBG, and the authors of the ENDO 2025 chart review wrote that free testosterone measurement is essential in men with obesity because weight loss raises SHBG. A US cohort of 3,671 men tested for low testosterone found obese men averaged an SHBG of 36 nmol/L versus 50 in non-obese men (Cooper and colleagues, 2015), so SHBG earns a place on every panel. We read total testosterone, free testosterone, SHBG, LH and estradiol together with your metabolic markers. Our functional health review adds the wider picture.

Frequently asked questions

Do GLP-1 drugs like semaglutide and tirzepatide raise or lower testosterone in men?

In men with overweight or obesity, the research points up. A 2026 meta-analysis in Andrology (8 studies, 375 men) found that GLP-1 drugs significantly raised total and calculated free testosterone along with LH and FSH. A 2025 Andrology meta-analysis (7 studies, 680 men) and a 2026 Journal of Sexual Medicine review (10 studies, 639 men) also found higher total testosterone. A chart review of 110 men presented at ENDO 2025 saw average total testosterone climb from 322 to 380 ng/dL on semaglutide, dulaglutide or tirzepatide. Most studies are small, short and in men with obesity or diabetes, so we measure your own numbers before and during treatment and track the change with you.

Does tirzepatide affect testosterone levels?

In men with obesity, tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) appears to raise testosterone levels, mostly alongside the weight lost, but the big SURMOUNT trials did not measure it. Testosterone is not among the registered outcomes of SURMOUNT-1 (2,539 adults without diabetes, a 72-week trial), which reported average weight loss of 15.0 to 20.9 percent across the three doses. In a two-month controlled pilot of 83 men with obesity and low testosterone (La Vignera and colleagues, 2025), the tirzepatide group had higher total, free and bioavailable testosterone, LH and FSH than men on diet and walking alone or on testosterone gel, and lower estradiol. A 2026 review of incretin drugs including tirzepatide reported average total testosterone rises of 2.5 to 5.2 nmol/L. We check your total and free testosterone, SHBG and LH before and during treatment.

Does semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) lower testosterone?

Measured testosterone went up, not down, in the controlled studies so far. In a 24-week randomized trial of 25 men with type 2 diabetes, obesity and low testosterone (Gregorič and colleagues, 2025), semaglutide 1 mg raised median total testosterone from 6.1 to 7.8 nmol/L and left LH and FSH unchanged, while testosterone injections in the comparison group lowered sperm counts. The STEP 1 trial (1,961 adults) did not measure testosterone. A US health-records study of 3,094 men aged 18 to 50 on semaglutide found more new records of testosterone deficiency (1.53 versus 0.80 percent) and erectile dysfunction than in matched men, an association that does not show the drug caused it. Free testosterone can lag when SHBG rises, so we read total, free and SHBG together.

Are there male-specific considerations with Saxenda (liraglutide) for weight management?

Yes. In men, the questions are what Saxenda does to testosterone, LH, FSH and sperm, and the small studies so far point up. In a randomized 16-week trial of 30 men with obesity and low testosterone (Jensterle and colleagues, 2019), liraglutide 3.0 mg daily raised total testosterone by 2.6 nmol/L (5.9 on testosterone gel), raised LH and FSH, and took off 7.9 kg. In a four-month study of 110 men aged 18 to 35 with metabolic hypogonadism (La Vignera and colleagues, 2023), men on liraglutide 3 mg daily had higher total testosterone, SHBG, LH and FSH than men on gonadotropins or testosterone gel, with better sperm parameters and erectile function. The SCALE trial behind the 3.0 mg dose (3,731 adults) was 78.5 percent women and did not report testosterone. We check your total and free testosterone, SHBG and LH before you start and as the weight comes off.

Is the testosterone rise from the drug itself or from the weight loss?

Researchers read it two ways, and both agree on the direction. The 2026 Corona meta-analysis found that the testosterone rises were independent of the weight lost, which suggests a direct effect on the hypothalamus-pituitary-testis axis. A 2026 systematic review of incretin therapies (La Vignera and Condorelli) reported rises of 2.5 to 5.2 nmol/L and concluded they were largely mediated by weight loss and better insulin sensitivity. A 2025 Andrology meta-analysis found that larger percent weight loss went with a larger testosterone rise. Either way, the practical step is the same: check your baseline, then re-check as the weight comes off.

Can fast weight loss on a GLP-1 drug cost me muscle?

Some of the weight lost is lean mass, and you can plan for it. In a body-composition substudy of SURMOUNT-1 (160 adults, 73 percent women), tirzepatide cut body weight by 21.3 percent, fat mass by 33.9 percent and lean mass by 10.9 percent over a 72-week trial, and about three-quarters of the weight lost was fat and one quarter was lean mass. A 2025 joint advisory from four US obesity and nutrition societies lists muscle and bone loss among the challenges of GLP-1 treatment and recommends resistance training and an appropriate diet to preserve muscle and bone. We build your protein, strength-training, sleep and fueling plan and track how your body responds.

Why can my total testosterone rise while free testosterone barely moves?

SHBG, the protein that carries testosterone in the blood, tends to rise as weight falls, and that pulls free testosterone down relative to total. In the 2026 Journal of Sexual Medicine review, free testosterone changes were inconsistent and often offset by rising SHBG. In the ENDO 2025 chart review, the authors stressed that free testosterone should be measured in men with obesity because SHBG moves with weight loss. We read total testosterone, free testosterone and SHBG together, so you see the whole picture.

Which labs should I get before and during GLP-1 treatment?

A useful panel covers total and free testosterone, SHBG, LH, FSH and estradiol, along with metabolic markers such as A1c. LH and FSH show whether the brain’s signal to the testes is staying on, and SHBG explains gaps between total and free testosterone. Many men repeat the panel after meaningful weight change.
