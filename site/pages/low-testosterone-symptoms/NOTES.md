## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 16 2026)
- Endocrine Society 2018 (Bhasin et al., JCEM 2018;103:1715, doi 10.1210/jc.2018-00229; abstract via PubMed E-utilities, full text via a hosted PDF copy of the JCEM article): diagnose hypogonadism only with symptoms/signs + unequivocally and consistently low T; fasting morning total T as initial test, confirm by repeating; free T when total near lower limit or SHBG-altering condition; look for the cause. Table 3: suggestive = reduced sexual desire and activity, decreased spontaneous erections/ED, breast discomfort/gynecomastia, infertility/low sperm count, height loss/low-trauma fracture/low BMD, hot flushes/sweats; nonspecific = decreased energy/motivation/initiative/self-confidence, sad or blue/depressed mood, poor concentration and memory, sleep disturbance/increased sleepiness, mild unexplained anemia, reduced muscle bulk and strength, increased body fat/BMI. Text: community surveys link low libido, ED, fatigue, irritability, depressed mood, poor concentration, reduced physical performance, sleep disturbance with low T; T affected by acute illness, nutritional deficiency, opioids, glucocorticoids, age, obesity, diabetes, OSA; morning peak, glucose/food suppress T; "30% of men with an initial T concentration in the hypogonadal range have a normal T concentration on repeat measurement" (page says "about three in ten").
- AUA 2018 testosterone deficiency guideline (Mulhall et al.; auanet.org public page, "Published 2018, Reviewed and Validity Confirmed 2024"): TD = low T plus symptoms/signs; symptoms "very non-specific and can be manifestations of other conditions (e.g., chronic fatigue, chronic stress, a depressed state)". Total T <300 ng/dL on two early-morning measurements (statement wording previously verified from the full text for /low-testosterone-age-or-weight NOTES).
- CUA 2021 (Grober et al., CUAJ 2021;15(5):E234-43, doi 10.5489/cuaj.7252; cua.org PDF): Table 1 sexual (decreased libido, delayed ejaculation, reduced ejaculate volume, decreased orgasm intensity, ED, loss of morning erections, infertility), cognitive/psychological (fatigue, mood changes, depression, insomnia, poor concentration/memory, irritable), physical (decreased vitality/energy, anemia, gynecomastia, hot flushes, decreased muscle mass and strength, increased visceral body fat, decreased BMD/osteopenia, testicular atrophy, hair loss). Signs not exclusive to TD; questionnaires lack specificity and should not substitute for history, exam and labs. Morning total T draw 7-11 am; calculated free/bioavailable T for equivocal results; total T <10 nmol/L reasonable threshold while some men have symptoms at higher levels. Potentially correctable causes to manage first: acute illness, obesity, opioids/glucocorticoids, extreme nutritional deficiency, extreme exercise, sleep apnea, hyperprolactinemia, hemochromatosis, pituitary disorders. Comorbidities: type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, obesity, osteoporosis, COPD, sleep apnea, HIV. Q14: rule out overlapping conditions (depression, hypothyroidism, sleep disorders). LH to classify primary vs secondary; prolactin when LH low/low-normal.
- EMAS, Wu et al., NEJM 2010;363:123-35 (doi 10.1056/NEJMoa0911101; PubMed abstract): random sample of 3,369 men aged 40-79 at eight European centres; morning total T by mass spectrometry; poor morning erection, low sexual desire, ED, inability to perform vigorous activity, depression and fatigue related to T; only the three sexual symptoms had a syndromic association; inverse relation between number of sexual symptoms and T; LOH defined as at least three sexual symptoms + total T <11 nmol/L + free T <220 pmol/L (cut-offs NOT used on the page).
- NHS "The 'male menopause'" (nhs.uk, page last reviewed 13 Oct 2022): symptom list (depression, low sex drive, ED, mood swings/irritability, loss of muscle and reduced ability to exercise, belly fat/gynaecomastia, lack of energy, insomnia/tiredness, poor concentration); "male menopause"/andropause called an unhelpful term; T falls steadily with age (page says "gradually", no figure); many cases have nothing to do with hormones (stress, depression, anxiety, lack of sleep, poor diet, lack of exercise, alcohol, smoking); ED physical causes include smoking and heart problems; late-onset hypogonadism diagnosed from symptoms + blood tests.
