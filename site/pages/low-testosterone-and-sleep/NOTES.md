## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 16 2026; PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities unless noted)
- Leproult & Van Cauter, JAMA 2011;305(21):2173-4, doi 10.1001/jama.2011.710 (PMC4445839 text via search excerpt + CDC stacks PDF): 10 healthy men, mean age 24.3, BMI 23.5; 3 nights of 10-h bedtimes then 8 nights of 5-h bedtimes; total sleep 8h55 -> 4h48; after the 7th restricted night, 8 AM-10 PM T 16.5 vs 18.4 nmol/L (P=.049), 2-10 PM 15.5 vs 17.9; "decreased by 10% to 15%"; vigor 28 -> 19; cortisol similar; authors: low energy, reduced libido, poor concentration, sleepiness can be produced by sleep deprivation. Page: "a week of five-hour nights", "10 to 15 percent", "just under nine hours to under five", 28 to 19. ("at least 15% of US working population" NOT used.)
- Penev, Sleep 2007;30(4):427-32, doi 10.1093/sleep/30.4.427: 12 healthy men 64-74; PSG total sleep time independent predictor of morning total and free T.
- Wittert, Asian J Androl 2014;16(2):262-5, doi 10.4103/1008-682X.122586: T peaks during sleep, nadir late afternoon, ultradian pulses every 90 min (LH); rise is sleep- rather than circadian-dependent and requires at least 3 h of sleep with normal architecture; OSA no direct effect on T after adjusting for age and obesity (possible indirect via obesity); CPAP does not reliably raise T; weight loss raises T predictably and linearly with weight lost; low T may affect overall sleep quality. (The review's line that T replacement improves sleep quality and that T treatment only transiently worsens OSA is NOT used.)
- Luboshitzky et al., JCEM 2001;86(3):1134-9, doi 10.1210/jcem.86.3.7296: 10 men, 7/13 ultrashort sleep-wake paradigm; fragmented sleep delayed the T rise (03:24 vs 22:35); rise linked to first REM; considerable attenuation only in men with no REM (rise seen in 4/10 with REM). Page: "the rise came hours later, and in men who reached no REM sleep it was much weaker".
- Axelsson et al., JCEM 2005;90(8):4530-5, doi 10.1210/jc.2005-0520: 7 healthy men 22-32; night sleep 23-07 vs day sleep 07-15; T rose log-linearly across sleep (15.3 -> 25.3 nmol/L night, 17.3 -> 26.4 day) and fell with time awake; circadian effect marginal. Page: "about 17 to 26 by day, about 15 to 25 by night".
- Cignarelli et al., Front Endocrinol 2019;10:551, doi 10.3389/fendo.2019.00551: OSA a frequent complication of obesity associated with hypogonadism; 12 studies (10 cohort + 2 RCT), 388 patients; CPAP not associated with significant change in total T (MD 1.08 nmol/L, 95% CI -0.48 to 2.64) or other outcomes.
- Balasubramanian et al., Urology 2020;138:52-9, doi 10.1016/j.urology.2019.10.040: 2,571 men at an andrology clinic (Houston); 619 (24.1%) nonstandard shifts; 196 (31.7%) of those high risk for SWSD; SWSD -> qADAM 5.47 points lower and T 100.4 ng/dL lower (adjusted for age, comorbidities, prior T supplementation). Page: "about one in four", "nearly a third", "about 100 ng/dL lower".
- Endocrine Society 2018 (Bhasin, JCEM 2018;103:1715; full text /tmp/es2018.txt): T affected by sleep disorders including OSA; fasting morning total T, repeat to confirm; morning peak, food lowers T; recommendation 2.2 against starting T therapy with untreated severe OSA (page: "advises against starting testosterone therapy while severe sleep apnea is untreated").
- CUA 2021 (Grober, CUAJ 2021;15(5):E234-43; full text /tmp/cua.txt): sleep apnea in causes / potentially correctable causes; "persistent sleep deprivation/restriction lowering testosterone levels by approximately 10-15%. A commitment to a structured sleep program may negate the impact of limited sleep"; exercise incl. resistance training -> relatively modest increases; morning draw 7-11 am.
- AUA 2018 (auanet.org): total T <300 ng/dL on two early-morning tests + symptoms (same short attributed fact as other answer pages).
- NHLBI (nhlbi.nih.gov, fetched Oct 16 2026): How much sleep is enough (adults 7-9 h; updated 2022-03-24); Healthy sleep habits (same bed/wake times, weekends within about an hour, quiet hour before bed without bright screens/intense exercise, no heavy meals or alcohol before bed, avoid nicotine/caffeine, caffeine effects up to 8 hours, daily outdoor time + activity, quiet/cool/dark bedroom; shift workers: naps + more time for sleep, bright lights at work, limit shift changes, caffeine in first part of shift, remove light/noise for day sleep); Sleep apnea symptoms (breathing that starts and stops, loud snoring, gasping, daytime sleepiness, sexual dysfunction or decreased libido; sleep study to diagnose).

### New facts (checked 2026-10-22, PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities efetch; saved /workspace/research-sleep1022/abs1.txt)
- Barrett-Connor E et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2008;93(7):2602-9, doi 10.1210/jc.2007-2622, PMID 18413429 (MrOS): 1,312 community-dwelling men 65+, baseline total T then actigraphy + in-home PSG; lower T -> lower sleep efficiency, more nocturnal awakenings, less slow-wave sleep, higher AHI, more time SpO2 <90; T unrelated to sleep duration; associations attenuated or absent after adjusting for BMI/waist ("largely explained by adiposity").
- Auyeung TW et al., J Am Med Dir Assoc 2015;16(7):630.e1-6, doi 10.1016/j.jamda.2015.04.006, PMID 25959075: 1,274 men >65 (Hong Kong), morning T by mass spectrometry; inverted-U, T rose with sleep duration up to 9.9 h; neither prolonged sleep latency nor insomnia complaint associated with T. Page: "up to about 10 hours".
- Agrawal P et al., Int J Impot Res 2024;36(1):78-82, doi 10.1038/s41443-022-00649-2, PMID 36473958: TriNetX US claims, men 40-70, propensity matched; T deficiency more likely with sleep apnea OR 1.66, insomnia OR 1.74, circadian rhythm dysfunction OR 2.63. Association only (page says so). Page wording "US health records" (claims database); avoids the word "diagnosed".
- Shigehara K et al., Aging Male 2018;21(2):99-105, doi 10.1080/13685538.2017.1378320, PMID 28920756 (EARTH subanalysis): hypogonadal men without OSA with sleep disturbance (AMS Q4 >=3), 48 analysed (24 TRT, 24 control); after 12 months significant differences in sleep disturbance, erectile symptoms, sexual desire, some SF-36 domains. Page: "a year of testosterone therapy improved sleep-disturbance scores against no treatment".
- Liu PY et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2003;88(8):3605-13, doi 10.1210/jc.2003-030236, PMID 12915643: 17 healthy men >60, randomized double-blind crossover, high-dose IM testosterone esters; total sleep time reduced ~1 h, hypoxemia +~5 min/night, RDI +~7 events/h; sleepiness/mood/QoL unchanged. Page: "cut sleep by about an hour and worsened breathing in sleep".
- Hoyos CM et al., Clin Endocrinol 2012;77(4):599-607, doi 10.1111/j.1365-2265.2012.04413.x, PMID 22512435: 67 obese men with severe OSA, hypocaloric diet + TU 1000 mg or placebo; ODI worse by 10.3 events/h and T90 by 6.1 at the 7-week PSG, not significant at 18 weeks. Page: "early in treatment; the gap faded by the end of the trial" (no week counts in prose per banned-claim scan).
- Endocrine Society 2018 OSA recommendation already sourced above.
- Not used: Mendelian randomization (Yuan 2023, inconsistent in men); TRAVERSE / T Trials (no verified sleep or insomnia outcome found, so the page makes no claim about them).
