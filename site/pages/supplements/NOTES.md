## Compliance flags (human / legal review)
1. **Title is 63 chars** and **the description reads as a benefit claim** (see SEO).
2. **Removed from the old page**: "We Do Not Practice Medicine", "Most supplements don't work. Yours probably don't either", dose/stack/detox/"ATP support"/"energy pathways" claims, "herbs for specific conditions", "detox protocols" and "cleanse" language, "practitioner-grade beats retail" and "research-leaning doses" claims, "results: bad interactions, blame on pills", "Book a Functional Assessment", and all Calgary wording. No condition, dose, mechanism or outcome claims remain.
3. **Regulatory wording (check against Health Canada NHP and FDA DSHEA / FTC rules):** the page states (a) in Canada natural health products carry an NPN on the label (accurate in general), (b) in the USA supplements aren't approved by the FDA before sale (accurate in general), (c) the bottom disclaimer uses DSHEA-style language "not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease". Confirm that wording and whether a US-style structure/function disclaimer is required for any product you sell or recommend.
4. **Does Odin sell or dispense supplements?** Unknown. Copy only says "what we suggest / your plan"; it never says you sell, stock or ship products and has no price or product claims. If you sell products, add the required disclosures. If affiliates or commissions exist, they must be disclosed.
5. **Who builds the plan** (a clinician? naturopath? herbalist?) and who may recommend herbs in each province/state is not stated. Copy says "we" and encourages sharing the plan with a physician or pharmacist. Confirm titles, licences and scope of practice, especially for herbs and for cross-border clients.
6. **Herb safety statements** ("can interact with prescription medication, affect blood pressure, blood clotting or mood") are general and widely accepted but unsourced; add citations or sign-off. "Evidence for many of these products is limited" is a general statement about "testosterone booster" products; same.
7. **Emergency line** in the safety section: "hives, swelling, trouble breathing or chest pain, call 911" (US/Canada).
8. **Fertility line** ("say so before starting anything hormone-related") and "never start, stop or change a medication because of a supplement" are safety nudges, keep.
9. **Graphics** all labelled "Illustration only"; the tray, layers and scan are about process. None implies results, none shows numbers.
10. **Disclaimer**: educational, not diagnosis or medical advice, supplements not a treatment for low testosterone, talk to physician or pharmacist (especially on medication), physician-only for medical items, 911/988.
11. No testimonials, statistics, "results in X weeks", urgency or scarcity (scanned: only negations such as "isn't a treatment" and "claims to treat or cure" appear).
12. **Shared nav/footer** still carry Calgary wording where the owner kept it; the page body has none.
