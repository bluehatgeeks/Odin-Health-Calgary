Why random stacks tend to fall short

Most supplement routines start from a trend, a friend or a label. That can leave you with a cupboard full of products, no clear way to tell what is helping, and no one checking how they fit with your medications.

  • {{CI:labs:0}}

    Dose

    Label doses, or double the label, without knowing whether you need it or how your body handles it.

  • {{CI:stress:1}}

    Overlap

    Several products can contain the same ingredient, and some ingredients interact with each other or with medication.

  • {{CI:clock:2}}

    Order

    Starting everything in the same week makes it impossible to tell what is doing what, or what is causing a side effect.

  • {{CI:food:3}}

    Food

    Capsules don’t replace a food plan. Sleep, meals, training and stress shape how you feel too.

A structured plan doesn’t promise a result. It makes the process clearer and easier to review.

Herbology, used with care

Herbal traditions, including Ayurveda and Western herbalism, have been used for centuries. “Natural” doesn’t mean risk-free. Herbs contain active compounds that can interact with prescription medication, affect blood pressure, blood clotting or mood, and may not suit certain conditions or an upcoming surgery.

  • {{CI:leaf:0}}

    Where it may fit

    When your history, symptoms and preferences point to a particular herb and it has been checked against your medications. For a set period, then reviewed.

  • {{CI:doc:1}}

    What we ask first

    Your medications, conditions, allergies, any planned surgery, and whether you plan to have children.

  • {{CI:shield:2}}

    What we steer away from

    Kitchen-sink blends, “testosterone booster” promises, and anything that claims to treat or cure a condition. Evidence for many of these products is limited.

What to look for in a product

Quality varies widely, and supplements are regulated differently from medicines. These are worth checking before you buy anything.

  • {{CI:labs:0}}

    Independent testing

    Look for a product tested by an independent third party for what is on the label and for contaminants.

  • {{CI:doc:1}}

    Licensing and labels

    In Canada, natural health products carry a Natural Product Number (NPN) on the label. In the USA, supplements aren’t approved by the FDA before they’re sold, so the label is the maker’s own statement unless a third party has tested it.

  • {{CI:caps:2}}

    A full ingredient list

    Amounts for each ingredient, not “proprietary blends”. Be wary of products that promise to treat, cure or boost a hormone.
