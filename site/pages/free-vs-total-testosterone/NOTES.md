## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 16 2026; PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities efetch, saved in /workspace/research-freet/)
- Endocrine Society 2018 (Bhasin, JCEM 2018;103(5):1715-44, doi 10.1210/jc.2018-00229; full text /tmp/es2018.txt): most circulating T bound to SHBG and albumin and to a lesser extent CBG and orosomucoid; "only 2.0% to 4.0% of circulating T is unbound or free" (page: "2 to 4 percent"); bioavailable T = unbound + albumin-bound, albumin-bound dissociable at capillary level in tissues with long transit times (liver, brain); free hormone hypothesis; Table 2 decreased SHBG: obesity, diabetes mellitus, glucocorticoids/some progestins/androgenic steroids, nephrotic syndrome, hypothyroidism, acromegaly, SHBG gene polymorphisms; increased SHBG: aging, HIV, cirrhosis and hepatitis, hyperthyroidism, some anticonvulsants, estrogens, polymorphisms; measure FT when SHBG altered or TT near lower limit (e.g. 200-400 ng/dL); TT far below (e.g. <150 ng/dL) FT generally not necessary; FT by equilibrium dialysis or formula using TT, SHBG, albumin that reflects dialysis; direct analog FT immunoassays inaccurate, should not be used; local labs usually lack accurate FT assays, use reliable reference lab; similar large variability with FT measurements; SHBG dimer / allosteric models approximate dialysis, preferred routine method needs further investigation; CDC-certified assays, LC-MS/MS higher specificity/precision esp. low range; harmonized TT 264-916 ng/dL (9.2-31.8 nmol/L) nonobese men 19-39 (2.5th-97.5th pct); harmonized FT range not established, lab lower limits may be used; EMAS sexual symptoms with TT <320 ng/dL (11 nmol/L) and FT <64 pg/mL (220 pmol/L); 30% of men with an initial low T have normal T on repeat (page: "30 percent"); morning fasting draw.
- Vermeulen A, Verdonck L, Kaufman JM. JCEM 1999;84(10):3666-72, doi 10.1210/jcem.84.10.6079, PMID 10523012: sera with SHBG from low (hirsute women) to extremely high (hyperthyroidism); calculated FT and equilibrium dialysis "almost identical under all conditions studied, except during pregnancy"; analog FT "only a fraction" of dialysis/calculated, fraction varying with SHBG; FAI (100T/SHBG) ratio varied with SHBG; neither aFT nor FAI reliable. (No default albumin value is quoted anywhere on the page.)
- Wu FC et al (EMAS). NEJM 2010;363(2):123-35, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa0911101, PMID 20554979: 3,369 men 40-79, eight European centres, TT by mass spectrometry, FT by Vermeulen formula; LOH = at least three sexual symptoms (poor morning erection, low sexual desire, erectile dysfunction) with TT <11 nmol/L (3.2 ng/mL) and FT <220 pmol/L (64 pg/mL).
- Antonio L et al (EMAS). JCEM 2016;101(7):2647-57, doi 10.1210/jc.2015-4106, PMID 26909800: 3,334 men 40-79; groups 2,641 normal TT/normal cFT (cFT 326 +/- 74 pmol/L), 277 normal TT/low cFT (older, poorer health, higher SHBG and LH, more sexual and physical symptoms, lower hemoglobin and bone ultrasound), 96 low TT/normal cFT (younger, more obese, lower SHBG, normal LH, features lacking), 320 low/low; thresholds TT >=10.5 nmol/L and cFT >=220 pmol/L; conclusion: low cFT even with normal TT associated with symptoms.
- Feldman HA et al (MMAS). JCEM 2002;87(2):589-98, doi 10.1210/jcem.87.2.8201, PMID 11836290: men 40-70 at baseline; follow-up data cross-sectional trends: total T -0.8%/yr of age, free and albumin-bound T about -2%/yr, SHBG +1.6%/yr. Page: "about 1.6 percent higher for each year of age ... about 2 percent lower ... 0.8 percent lower".
- Ding EL et al. NEJM 2009;361(12):1152-63, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa0804381, PMID 19657112: SHBG inversely associated with insulin resistance (background); low SHBG strong predictor of type 2 diabetes in women and men (replicated in men). (The cohort name containing "Physicians" is NOT used on the page.)
- CUA 2021 (Grober, CUAJ 2021;15(5):E234-43, doi 10.5489/cuaj.7252; full text /tmp/cua.txt): gold standards equilibrium dialysis and mass spectrometry; total T morning draw 7-11 am best initial test; calculated free or bioavailable T from TT, SHBG, albumin to resolve equivocal TT in symptomatic men; TT <10 nmol/L reasonable threshold; Q14 symptoms with "normal" T: rule out overlapping conditions (depression, hypothyroidism, sleep disorders); elevated SHBG can effectively neutralize circulating testosterone; in equivocal cases SHBG + calculated free/bioavailable T may give additional insight.
