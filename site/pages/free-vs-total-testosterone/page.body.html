What is the difference between free and total testosterone?

Short answer

Total testosterone counts all the testosterone in your blood. Free testosterone is the small part that is not bound to a protein — only 2 to 4 percent, according to the Endocrine Society. Most is held by SHBG (tightly) and albumin (loosely). When SHBG runs high or low, or your total T sits near the lower limit, free T can tell a different story. Total, free and bioavailable testosterone

The total testosterone on your lab report is the sum of everything in circulation. According to the Endocrine Society’s 2018 guideline, most of it is bound to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and albumin, a little to cortisol-binding globulin and orosomucoid, and only 2 to 4 percent travels unbound, or free. “Bioavailable” testosterone is the free part plus the part loosely held by albumin, which lets go easily in tissues with slow blood flow such as the liver and brain. Under the free hormone hypothesis, it is the unbound hormone that gets into cells and acts. So two men with the same total T can have different amounts of usable testosterone if their SHBG differs.

SHBG and testosterone: what raises and lowers it

SHBG is the main carrier. When it runs high, more testosterone is held tightly, so total T can look normal while free T is low. When it runs low, total T can read low while free T is fine. The Endocrine Society lists what moves it. Lower SHBG: obesity, diabetes, an underactive thyroid, nephrotic syndrome, acromegaly, and glucocorticoids, some progestins and androgenic steroids. Higher SHBG: aging, cirrhosis and hepatitis, an overactive thyroid, HIV, some anticonvulsants and estrogens. Gene variants can push it either way. Age shows the pattern clearly: in follow-up data from the Massachusetts Male Aging Study (Feldman and colleagues, 2002), SHBG was about 1.6 percent higher for each year of age, while free and albumin-bound testosterone were about 2 percent lower and total T only 0.8 percent lower. Low SHBG also travels with insulin resistance: in a 2009 New England Journal of Medicine study by Ding and colleagues, low SHBG strongly predicted type 2 diabetes in women and men. Calculated free testosterone: total T, SHBG and albumin

Most free T results are calculated, not measured. The Endocrine Society says free T should be measured by equilibrium dialysis or estimated with a formula that uses total testosterone, SHBG and albumin and accurately reflects dialysis. The best-known formula comes from Vermeulen, Verdonck and Kaufman (Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism, 1999). They tested it on samples with SHBG ranging from very low to extremely high, and calculated free T was almost identical to dialysis in every condition studied except pregnancy. A calculation is only as good as its inputs, so it needs an accurate total T (the guideline favours CDC-certified assays, and mass spectrometry offers higher precision at low levels), plus SHBG and albumin from the same sample.

Dialysis, calculated or a direct analog test?

Equilibrium dialysis is the reference method: serum sits against a membrane that only unbound testosterone can cross. It is accurate but slow, so it is usually sent to a reference lab. Direct analog immunoassays are the quick free T tests many local labs offer. In Vermeulen’s study their values were only a fraction of the dialysis result, and that fraction shifted with SHBG, so the error is biggest exactly when free T matters most. The Endocrine Society says these direct analog tests are inaccurate and should not be used, and advises a reliable reference lab for free T. The same study found the free androgen index (testosterone divided by SHBG) also varied with SHBG and was not a reliable index. Look for the method name on your report: “calculated” or “equilibrium dialysis” is what you want to see.

Units: nmol/L vs ng/dL, pmol/L vs pg/mL

The Canadian Urological Association guideline writes total T in nmol/L; the Endocrine Society and American Urological Association write it in ng/dL. Testosterone weighs 288.4 grams per mole (PubChem), so 1 nmol/L is about 28.8 ng/dL, and for free T 1 pg/mL is about 3.47 pmol/L. The Endocrine Society’s own pairs line up: 264 ng/dL is 9.2 nmol/L, 320 ng/dL is 11 nmol/L, and a free T of 64 pg/mL is 220 pmol/L. To compare, multiply nmol/L by 28.8 to get ng/dL, and multiply free T in pg/mL by 3.47 to get pmol/L. Free T also sits in a far smaller unit than total T (a picomole is a thousandth of a nanomole), so a free T of 300 is not “higher” than a total T of 15. Always read the unit next to the number.

What is a normal free testosterone for men?

There is no single agreed number. The Endocrine Society says a harmonized free T range has not been established, so ranges vary with the dialysis method or formula, and labs set their own lower limits. Total T now has one: 264 to 916 ng/dL (9.2 to 31.8 nmol/L) in healthy, non-obese men aged 19 to 39, for CDC-certified assays. The free T figure researchers use most comes from the European Male Ageing Study (Wu and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, 2010; 3,369 men aged 40 to 79). It defined late-onset hypogonadism as at least three sexual symptoms (poor morning erections, low sexual desire, erectile dysfunction) with total T below 11 nmol/L and free T below 220 pmol/L (64 pg/mL). In a later analysis of the same men, calculated free T averaged 326 pmol/L in those with normal total and free T. The Canadian Urological Association treats total T below 10 nmol/L as a reasonable threshold, and the American Urological Association uses below 300 ng/dL on two early-morning tests with symptoms. We read the range on your own report, the method behind it and your symptoms together.

Total testosterone normal but you still have symptoms?

This is where free T earns its place. In a 2016 European Male Ageing Study analysis (Antonio and colleagues, Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism; 3,334 men aged 40 to 79), 277 men had a normal total T but a low calculated free T. They were older and in poorer health, had higher SHBG and LH, and reported more sexual and physical symptoms, with lower hemoglobin and bone scores. The 96 men with low total T but normal free T were younger and heavier, with lower SHBG, and lacked the features of low testosterone. The authors concluded that low free T, even with a normal total, goes with symptoms. The Canadian Urological Association adds two points: look-alike conditions such as depression, an underactive thyroid and sleep disorders should be ruled out, and high SHBG can effectively neutralize circulating testosterone, so in unclear cases SHBG with a calculated free or bioavailable T adds insight. When free testosterone is worth checking

Start with the basics: a fasting, early-morning total T, repeated on another morning, because the Endocrine Society notes that 30 percent of men with one low result are normal on the repeat. The guideline then recommends free T in two situations: when total T sits near the lower limit (for example 200 to 400 ng/dL), and when a condition that changes SHBG is present. When total T is far below the limit (for example under 150 ng/dL), free T is unlikely to be normal, so it is generally not needed. Symptoms with a normal total T are a third reason, as the European Male Ageing Study data above show. See low testosterone labs to ask for and when to get checked for low T.
