How to raise testosterone naturally: what actually works

Short answer

The changes with the best evidence are losing extra body fat, sleeping enough and staying active. In a meta-analysis of 24 studies, low-calorie diets raised total testosterone by 2.87 nmol/L on average (about 83 ng/dL) and bariatric surgery by 8.73 nmol/L. A week of five-hour nights cut young men’s daytime testosterone by 10 to 15 percent. Cutting back heavy drinking, managing stress and reviewing medications such as opioids and steroids matter too. Vitamin D and zinc help mainly when you are low, and most “testosterone booster” ingredients have little or no human evidence. What actually raises testosterone without TRT?

Guidelines on both sides of the border start in the same place. The American Urological Association (2018) says every man with low testosterone should be counselled on lifestyle, because losing weight and moving more can raise total testosterone. The Canadian Urological Association (2021) says fixable causes should be managed before testosterone therapy: obesity, opioids and glucocorticoids, extreme dieting or exercise, and sleep apnea. The Endocrine Society (2018) calls these functional causes. Here is how the levers compare. For the bigger decision, see TRT, or fix the causes first?

Sleep: protect the hours your body makes testosterone

Most of the day’s testosterone release happens during sleep. In the best-known test, Leproult and Van Cauter at the University of Chicago (JAMA, 2011) gave 10 healthy men averaging 24 years old three nights of 10-hour bedtimes, then eight nights of five-hour bedtimes. Daytime testosterone fell from 18.4 to 16.5 nmol/L, a drop the authors put at 10 to 15 percent,. For scale, they noted that normal ageing lowers testosterone by 1 to 2 percent a year. The Canadian Urological Association says a structured sleep program may offset the loss. Loud snoring, gasping at night and daytime sleepiness point to sleep apnea, covered below. The full picture is in low testosterone and sleep.

Losing body fat: the biggest lever for most men

Extra body fat dampens the brain’s LH signal to the testes and converts more testosterone to estradiol, the Canadian Urological Association explains. The good news runs the other way. In a meta-analysis of 24 studies (Corona and colleagues, 2013), total testosterone rose by 2.87 nmol/L on average after low-calorie diets (about 83 ng/dL) and by 8.73 nmol/L after bariatric surgery (about 250 ng/dL). The more weight men lost, the bigger the rise, and gains were larger in younger men without diabetes. In the European Male Ageing Study, which followed 2,736 men aged 40 to 79 for about four years, weight loss went with a proportional rise in testosterone and weight gain with a proportional fall. If your weight sits mostly around the middle, start here: see is low T age or weight? and, if you use semaglutide or tirzepatide, weight-loss drugs and testosterone.

Exercise: lift for muscle, move for testosterone

Exercise helps, but less than the internet suggests: the Canadian Urological Association calls the rises relatively modest. A 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies in men 60 and older (Hayes and Elliott) found small, significant rises in resting testosterone with endurance and interval training, and no significant change with resistance training. Lifting still earns its place: it builds the muscle low T erodes and helps keep lost fat off. In a Japanese program of 41 men with overweight or obesity (Kumagai and colleagues, 2016), testosterone rose over twelve weeks of aerobic training and calorie cuts, and the men who added the most daily steps gained the most. One caution: very hard training on too little food works against you.

Alcohol: how much is too much for testosterone?

In a diet-controlled Dutch trial, 10 healthy middle-aged men drank beer with dinner (about 40 g of alcohol a day) for three weeks and alcohol-free beer for three weeks; testosterone was 6.8 percent lower after the beer period (Sierksma and colleagues, 2004). A 2023 review (Smith and colleagues) found that small amounts can briefly raise testosterone, but heavy drinking, especially over the long term, lowers its production. The Endocrine Society and the Canadian Urological Association both list alcohol abuse among the causes of low testosterone, and a late drink costs you sleep too.

Stress, cortisol and under-eating

Stress lowers testosterone mainly through the brain. A 2026 review (Friedl and colleagues) of military, athlete and competition studies found that severe energy deficits, broken sleep and stress you cannot control turn down the brain’s GnRH and LH signals and often raise SHBG, which cuts free testosterone even more. Heavy lifting did not lower resting testosterone when men ate enough, and levels came back with food, sleep and recovery. Cortisol is not the whole story: in the JAMA sleep study, testosterone fell without any rise in cortisol. What helps: eat enough to fuel your training, protect sleep, and build in recovery.

Medications, steroids and sleep apnea

Some of the most fixable causes sit in the medicine cabinet. The Endocrine Society notes that opioids at doses equivalent to 30 mg of methadone suppress testosterone, and longer-acting opioids more so; a meta-analysis of 17 studies found testosterone was lower in men who use opioids regularly, whatever the type, methadone included (Bawor and colleagues, 2015). Glucocorticoids such as prednisone, anabolic steroids (during use and after stopping) and marijuana are on the Canadian list too. We help you list everything you take so any change is planned safely with whoever manages each medication. Sleep apnea is common with obesity and linked with low testosterone, but in a meta-analysis of 12 studies and 388 men, CPAP on its own did not significantly change total testosterone (Cignarelli and colleagues, 2019). Treat apnea for sleep and energy; weight loss is what moves testosterone.

Vitamin D and zinc: only if you are low

Vitamin D: in an Austrian trial of 54 men on a weight-loss program whose vitamin D started in the deficient range, a year of 3,332 IU a day raised total testosterone from 10.7 to 13.4 nmol/L, with no change on placebo (Pilz, 2011). Two later trials, in men with normal and with low testosterone, found no effect (Lerchbaum, 2017 and 2019), and a 2026 meta-analysis found no clear effect. Zinc: severe and moderate zinc deficiency goes with low testosterone. In nine older men who were marginally deficient, six months of zinc raised testosterone from 8.3 to 16.0 nmol/L (Prasad, 1996), while a zinc and magnesium (ZMA) supplement did nothing in men already eating enough zinc (Koehler, 2009). The US NIH sets men’s zinc needs at 11 mg a day and the upper limit at 40 mg; long-term excess can lower copper. Test, then top up only what is low.

Do testosterone boosters work?

Mostly not as advertised. A University of Southern California team (Clemesha and colleagues, 2020) analysed 50 “T booster” products: 90 percent claimed to raise testosterone, yet of their 109 ingredients, 67 had no published data on testosterone at all, 27 had some data showing a rise (15 of those with conflicting data too) and 11 had data showing a fall. Thirteen products went over US upper limits for zinc, vitamin B3 or magnesium. Across 37 human studies of the top online boosters’ main ingredients, 30 percent saw a rise and 46 percent no effect (Balasubramanian, 2019). The Canadian Urological Association says boosters cannot be recommended and warns that some have contained banned substances, including steroids. A 2026 meta-analysis of 23 ashwagandha trials found lower cortisol and an average rise in men of about 57 ng/dL, and called for more standardized trials. Health Canada licenses natural health products; the US FDA does not approve supplements for safety or effectiveness before sale. Our supplement plans start from your labs, not a label.

How much change to expect

Natural changes move testosterone by a few nmol/L, not tens. Averages: low-calorie diets +2.87 nmol/L (about 83 ng/dL); bariatric surgery +8.73 nmol/L (about 250 ng/dL); one week of five-hour nights about −1.9 nmol/L (our arithmetic, 18.4 to 16.5); vitamin D across randomized trials +0.47 nmol/L, which was not a clear effect. For scale, the American Urological Association uses a total testosterone of 300 ng/dL (about 10.4 nmol/L) on two early-morning tests, with symptoms, as its line for low testosterone. A man just under that line who carries extra weight and sleeps badly has room to move; a lean man who sleeps well has less. That is why we test before and after.

When lifestyle isn’t enough

Lifestyle is the first step, not the only one. The Endocrine Society recommends confirming low testosterone with repeat morning tests and looking for the cause, because organic causes (pituitary or testicular disease, genetic conditions) will not respond to habits alone. A TRT conversation makes sense when your levels stay low on repeat morning tests after a fair try at the fixable causes and your symptoms persist, or when the numbers point to a problem in the pituitary or testes; LH and FSH help tell which.
