How much do TRT and testosterone cost in Canada vs the USA?

Short answer

TRT has two price tags: the testosterone itself and the lab testing around it. In Canada, provincial plans cover medically necessary lab tests, but public drug plans pay for testosterone only in narrow cases, so the usual routes are private insurance or paying at the pharmacy. As of October 2026, a generic 10 mL vial of testosterone cypionate is listed at $34.88 CAD on Ontario’s and Alberta’s drug lists and starts at $37.99 USD cash in the USA. Generic 1 percent gel is about $89 CAD a month at Ontario’s list price, and a generic 1.62 percent gel pump (88 g) is $55.73 to $77.13 USD cash; pellets cost more. The biggest swing in the USA is the clinic: in a 2022 study, cash-pay telehealth platforms charged $1,586 to $4,200 USD for a year of injections. What drives the yearly cost of TRT?

Four things set the bill. Lab tests: testosterone has to be confirmed before starting, and hematocrit and PSA are repeated at three and six months and then yearly, per British Columbia’s testing protocol. The form: the Endocrine Society calls injections “relatively inexpensive, if self-administered,” while daily gels and implanted pellets cost more. Coverage: who pays for labs and the drug depends on your province or state, your insurance and your age. Visits and clinic fees: in the USA these are often the largest line. Prices below were checked in October 2026 and vary by province, state and pharmacy. Before any of this, the first step is confirming low T: see when to get checked for low T.

Testosterone testing costs in Canada

Provincial plans pay for lab tests that are medically necessary. Ontario says OHIP covers medically necessary tests in community labs when you have a lab requisition; British Columbia’s plan paid labs $15.81 for a total testosterone and $29.37 for calculated bioavailable testosterone with SHBG (fees current to April 2022). Two catches. First, BC’s protocol exists partly to limit “wellness” and “anti-aging” testing and does not support testing men without symptoms. Second, PSA in Ontario is covered only when prostate cancer is suspected or being followed; otherwise you pay. Private-pay options, as of October 2026: the LifeLabs testosterone test (total, free and bioavailable T plus SHBG) is $98 in Ontario and BC and is not covered by OHIP, with bioavailable T as the paid part; a LifeLabs PSA is $71; and a Dynacare Plus home kit (total T, free T, SHBG) is $99 with an Ontario health card or $138.99 without, because OHIP pays for the lab work but not the kit, shipping or admin. Private insurance coverage of these tests varies by plan. Which markers are worth paying for is covered in labs to ask for.

Do provincial drug plans pay for TRT?

Mostly not for age-related low T. OHIP does not pay for drugs outside hospital. Ontario: the Ontario Drug Benefit covers people 65 and older and some other groups, and every testosterone product it pays for is Limited Use; code 397 requires low morning testosterone with documented hypothalamic, pituitary or testicular disease, or HIV, and adds that older men with nonspecific symptoms such as fatigue and a low-normal random level do not qualify. Seniors pay the first $100 each year, then up to $6.11 per fill. British Columbia: PharmaCare covers testosterone injections through Special Authority for listed conditions such as hypogonadism, removal of the testes, undescended testes, Klinefelter syndrome or pituitary disease. Alberta: generic cypionate injection is a regular benefit on the government drug list, gels are not a benefit, and the oral capsule’s criteria rule out “androgen decline in the aging male.” Everyone else leans on workplace or private plans, which gave 27 million people drug coverage according to the Canadian Life and Health Insurance Association’s 2025 factbook, or pays cash.

Injections, gels and pellets: how often you pay

The form decides how often you refill and what each refill costs. The Endocrine Society lists typical starting doses of 75 to 100 mg of testosterone enanthate or cypionate a week, or 150 to 200 mg every two weeks; 50 to 100 mg of 1 percent gel a day; and pellets of 600 to 1,200 mg placed under the skin through a small incision, lasting three to six months. Injections are the cheapest per dose but bring peaks and dips; gels give steadier levels but can pass to a partner by skin contact; pellets mean fewer visits but a minor procedure each time. Neither Ontario’s nor Alberta’s drug list includes a pellet product. Each form has its own side effects too, laid out in TRT side effects.

Testosterone prices: Canada vs the USA

Canada (CAD, government list prices, October 2026): Ontario lists generic testosterone cypionate at $34.88 per 10 mL vial (100 mg/mL), the same per-mL price Alberta lists ($3.4878 per mL); testosterone enanthate is $35.59 per 5 mL vial in Ontario; 1 percent gel is $2.96 per 5 g packet for the generic and $4.51 for brand AndroGel. Pharmacies add a dispensing fee, and Ontario does not cover brand Depo-Testosterone. USA (USD, cash prices on Drugs.com, October 2026, not valid with insurance): generic cypionate starts at $37.99 per 10 mL vial; brand Depo-Testosterone runs $44.70 to $93.29; generic 1.62 percent gel is $55.73 to $77.13 per 88 g pump; brand AndroGel 1 percent is $318.13 to $683.83 for 30 packets; and Testopel pellets are about $1,065 for ten 75 mg pellets, before the insertion visit. Generic injections list at similar dollar figures in both countries; brand gels and pellets are where US cash prices climb.

US lab costs: insurance, Medicare and cash

With insurance, your plan’s copays and deductible decide the bill. Medicare Part B pays the full cost of a PSA blood test once a year for men over 50. Without insurance, consumer lab sites post prices (October 2026): Quest lists total testosterone at $69, PSA at $69 and a complete blood count with hematocrit at $29, each plus a $6 service fee, and accepts HSA and FSA cards but does not bill insurance; Labcorp OnDemand lists a comprehensive testosterone panel (total and free T, SHBG, albumin) at $159, on sale for $119.25 when checked. A baseline of testosterone, PSA and hematocrit at Quest comes to $185 with fees. Labs only help if they are drawn right, so plan for two morning tests and the same lab each time.

US insurance vs cash-pay telehealth clinics

Cash-pay men’s clinics bundle visits, labs and medication into a monthly fee. A 2022 study in The Journal of Sexual Medicine (Jesse and colleagues) priced a year of injectable testosterone at three online platforms at $1,586 to $4,200, against $134.01 to $1,333.04 at a tertiary medical center billed through insurance or Medicare, and found gels cost more online too. The authors’ advice: men with private insurance or Medicare will likely pay more through these platforms. Price is not the only gap. In a secret-shopper study in JAMA Internal Medicine (Dubin and colleagues, 2022), 6 of 7 platforms offered testosterone to a man with normal total and free T who wanted children, and only one asked about heart events or fertility. Before paying a monthly fee, compare it with what your insurance covers and make sure the plan starts with confirmed low T and its causes, as in TRT or fix the causes first.

Ongoing monitoring costs

British Columbia’s protocol sets the rhythm: hematocrit, PSA and a prostate exam before starting, again at three and six months, then yearly once stable, with total testosterone checked yearly on a stable dose. Paid privately, one round of testosterone plus PSA at LifeLabs is $169 CAD, and testosterone, PSA and hematocrit at Quest is $185 USD with fees. That makes the first six months (three rounds) $507 CAD or $555 USD if every test is out of pocket, then about one round a year. In Canada, medically necessary tests on a requisition are paid by the provincial plan (PSA in Ontario is the exception above), so the real number is often far lower. That is our math from the listed prices, and it leaves out visit fees.

Putting it together: the drug cost for a year

A worked example with the listed prices (our arithmetic, drug only). A 10 mL vial at 100 mg/mL holds 1,000 mg. At 100 mg a week, the top of the Endocrine Society’s weekly range, one vial lasts ten weeks, so a year takes 5.2 vials: about $181 CAD at Ontario’s list price (about $15 a month) or about $198 USD at the lowest US cash price. Gel at 50 mg a day is one 5 g packet a day: about $1,079 CAD a year for the generic and $1,647 for the brand at Ontario prices. One Testopel insertion of ten pellets (750 mg) is about $1,065 USD before the visit. Add labs, dispensing fees and any clinic or visit fees on top, and remember your dose and form may differ.
