## Facts and where they come from (all checked Oct 15 2026)
- Massachusetts Male Aging Study (Feldman, JCEM 2002;87:589): 1,156 of 1,709 men (40-70 at baseline) followed 7-10 yr; total T -1.6%/yr, bioavailable T -2-3%/yr; apparent good health added 10-15% to several androgens; authors suggest incident poor health may accelerate decline.
- BLSA (Harman, JCEM 2001;86:724): 890 men, -0.124 nmol/L/yr; hypogonadal total-T values ~20% over 60, 30% over 70, 50% over 80.
- EMAS (Wu JCEM 2008;93:2737): 3,200 men 40-79; BMI>=30 total T -5.09 nmol/L, BMI 25-30 -2.32 vs BMI<25, LH unchanged/decreased; age -> lower free T with raised LH. EMAS longitudinal (Camacho EJE 2013;168:445): ~4.4 yr, mean annualised T change -0.1 nmol/L; weight loss -> proportional T rise, gain -> fall; free T changed only with >=15% weight change. EMAS subtypes (Tajar JCEM 2010;95:1810): 3,369 men; secondary 11.8%, primary 2.0%, compensated 9.5%; older age -> primary/compensated; BMI>=30 -> secondary.
- Corona EJE 2013;168:829: 24 studies; total T +2.87 nmol/L (low-calorie diet) vs +8.73 nmol/L (bariatric surgery); larger rise with more weight loss, younger age, no diabetes. (Participant count and mean weight-loss percentages NOT used: seen only in a secondary summary.)
- Cooper, Clin Endocrinol 2015;83:828: 3,671 men tested for low T; obese total T 10.5 vs 14.1 nmol/L, SHBG 36 vs 50, calculated free T no difference. Fui/Dupuis/Grossmann, Asian J Androl 2014;16:223: moderate obesity lowers total T via lower SHBG, severe obesity also lowers free T via HPT suppression; functional, non-permanent, can be reversible with substantial weight loss.
- Leproult & Van Cauter JAMA 2011;305:2173: 10 men, mean age 24.3, ~1 week of 5-h bedtimes; 8 AM-10 PM T 16.5 vs 18.4 nmol/L, 2-10 PM 15.5 vs 17.9; vigor 28 -> 19; cortisol profiles similar; letter notes OSA/fragmentation linked to lower T. CUA 2021 Q&A: persistent sleep restriction lowers T by ~10-15%, structured sleep program may offset; resistance training = relatively modest rises.
- Friedl, Nindl, Potter JCEM 2026;111:e1951 (abstract read): suppression under sustained stress commonly central, reversible; energy deficit, sleep disruption, uncontrollable psychogenic stress suppress GnRH/LH, raise SHBG (free T lowered more); heavy resistance training does not suppress basal T when energy availability is maintained.
- AUA 2018 (full text read): total T <300 ng/dL on two early-morning measurements + symptoms; LH in all men with low T (strong); statement 21 lifestyle counselling for all men (weight loss/activity can raise total T and reduce symptoms; weight-loss programs concurrent with therapy).
- TRAVERSE (Lincoff NEJM 2023;389:107): 5,246 men 45-80, symptoms, two fasting T <300 ng/dL (+ preexisting/high CV risk; the page says only "symptoms and two fasting readings under 300"). T4DM (Wittert Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol 2021;9:32): men 50-74 enrolled in a lifestyle programme and randomised to testosterone undecanoate or placebo; only that enrolment fact is used (no diabetes/haematocrit figures).
