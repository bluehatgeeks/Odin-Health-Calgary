Is my low testosterone from aging, or from weight, sleep and stress?

Short answer

Usually a mix, and part of the mix is changeable. Age brings a gradual average decline, about 1.6 percent a year in the Massachusetts Male Aging Study. Extra weight, short sleep and sustained stress can pull testosterone lower on top of that, and those are the parts you can work on. Two morning labs that include LH help show which pattern you have. What aging does to testosterone

Testosterone drifts down as men age, gradually and unevenly. In the Massachusetts Male Aging Study (1,156 men followed 7 to 10 years from ages 40 to 70), total testosterone fell about 1.6 percent a year and bioavailable testosterone 2 to 3 percent a year, and the authors suggested that new health problems may speed the decline. The Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging found an average drop of 0.124 nmol/L a year in 890 healthy men; by total testosterone criteria about one in five men over 60, three in ten over 70 and half over 80 had hypogonadal values. In the European Male Ageing Study, the average yearly change over about four years was only around 0.1 nmol/L.

What extra weight does

In the European Male Ageing Study (3,200 men aged 40 to 79), men with a BMI of 30 or more had total testosterone about 5.1 nmol/L lower than men under BMI 25, and men with a BMI of 25 to 30 were about 2.3 nmol/L lower, with LH unchanged or lower, which points to the brain’s signal rather than the testes. Obesity also lowers SHBG: in a US cohort of 3,671 men tested for low testosterone, obese men averaged 10.5 versus 14.1 nmol/L total testosterone, while calculated free testosterone did not differ. A review in the Asian Journal of Andrology describes obesity-linked low testosterone as functional and non-permanent, and it can be reversible.

Does losing weight bring testosterone back?

Often it raises it. A meta-analysis of 24 studies (Corona and colleagues, 2013) found that total testosterone rose by about 2.9 nmol/L on average after low-calorie diets and about 8.7 nmol/L after bariatric surgery, with bigger rises in men who lost more weight, were younger and did not have diabetes. In the European Male Ageing Study follow-up, weight loss tracked with a proportional rise in testosterone and weight gain with a proportional fall; free testosterone moved significantly in men who gained or lost 15 percent or more of their weight. The AUA guideline recommends counselling every man with low testosterone on weight loss and activity, and the Canadian guideline adds that resistance training gives relatively modest rises.

What short sleep does

In a University of Chicago study (Leproult and Van Cauter, JAMA 2011), 10 healthy men averaging 24 years old spent about a week on five-hour bedtimes. Their daytime testosterone averaged 16.5 nmol/L, against 18.4 nmol/L after rested nights, and 15.5 against 17.9 nmol/L between 2 and 10 PM. Their vigor scores fell from 28 to 19 over the week. The Canadian Urological Association guideline sums up persistent sleep restriction as lowering testosterone by roughly 10 to 15 percent and says a structured sleep program may offset it. The JAMA report also notes that sleep fragmentation and obstructive sleep apnea are linked with lower testosterone.

Stress, fueling and the brain’s signal

A 2026 review in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism (Friedl, Nindl and Potter) pulled together military field studies, endurance athletes and competition models. It concludes that testosterone suppression under sustained stress is commonly a centrally driven, reversible adaptation rather than testicular failure: severe energy deficit, sleep disruption and uncontrollable psychological stress turn down the hypothalamic signals that drive LH, and often raise SHBG, which lowers free testosterone disproportionately. The same review found that heavy resistance training did not suppress baseline testosterone when energy intake was sufficient.

How to tell which pattern you have

The AUA guideline defines low testosterone as total testosterone under 300 ng/dL on two early-morning measurements, together with symptoms, and recommends measuring LH in every man with a low result (strong recommendation). In the European Male Ageing Study (3,369 men aged 40 to 79), researchers sorted men by LH: low testosterone with low or normal LH (the secondary pattern) in 11.8 percent, low testosterone with high LH (the primary pattern) in 2.0 percent, and normal testosterone with raised LH (compensated) in 9.5 percent. Older age was linked to the primary and compensated patterns, and a BMI of 30 or more was strongly linked to the secondary pattern.
