Low testosterone, andropause, or just getting older?

Andropause is the everyday word for what many men feel when testosterone falls: less energy, less drive, a flatter mood, a softer body.

The clinical name is low testosterone, or late-onset hypogonadism. Getting older can be part of the story. It is rarely the whole story.

“Normal” and “terrible” can both be true.

Total and free testosterone are different tests.

Many standard panels report total testosterone only. Free testosterone, the part your body can actually use, is a separate measurement. A result inside the reference range doesn’t always settle the question. Ask which one was tested.

It isn’t always testosterone itself.

Something upstream is often pushing it down.

Weight, insulin resistance, stress, illness, poor sleep, some medications and other hormones can all suppress testosterone. Finding which one applies to you is the point of the work.

Seven reasons testosterone drops, and what each one means for you

We look at all seven with you, then build your plan around the ones that apply.

Four everyday drivers we build your plan around

  • Metabolic strain

    Extra belly fat, insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes can lower testosterone and make it harder to rebuild.

  • Stress, illness and under-recovery

    Long-running stress, illness, inflammation, under-eating or overtraining compete with testosterone for the body’s resources.

  • Medications and substances

    Some prescribed drugs and substances suppress testosterone. We help you spot which ones may be playing a part.

  • Other hormones getting in the way

    Changes in other hormones can reduce how much testosterone your body makes or can use.

Three deeper causes we look for

We look for these too, so your plan matches your real cause.

  • Testicular problems

    The testes themselves can’t make enough testosterone.

  • Pituitary or hypothalamic problems

    The brain’s signal to the testes isn’t getting through.

  • Structural or genetic conditions

    Inherited or physical conditions, found through careful testing.

Frequently asked questions

What is andropause, and is it the same as low testosterone?

Andropause is the everyday word for the drop in energy, drive and mood that some men notice as testosterone declines with age. The clinical name is late-onset hypogonadism, or low testosterone. It is not the same for every man, and not every man who feels this way has low testosterone.

How do I know if my symptoms are low testosterone?

Fatigue, low drive, poor sleep and low mood overlap with stress, sleep problems, thyroid issues and more, so the clearest answer comes from blood tests together with a full history. Our free assessment shows which pattern your symptoms resemble and where to look first, and your first call covers which tests are worth asking for.

My labs came back “normal.” Can low testosterone still be the problem?

It can be worth a second look. Many standard panels report total testosterone only, while free testosterone, the portion your body can actually use, is a separate measurement. A number inside the reference range doesn't always settle the question. It helps to ask which test was run, and we can walk you through it on a call.
