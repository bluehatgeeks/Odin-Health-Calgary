## The Financial Psychology: Why Emma Pays $750/Month

This is the most important section for understanding her buying decision.

### 1. She's already spending — just ineffectively
Emma's current monthly health spend before Odin Lab:
- Supplements (magnesium, B12, omega-3, probiotic, vitamin D): ~$80–$150/month
- Yoga or pilates membership: ~$120–$180/month
- Occasional naturopath visit: ~$100–$150/visit every few months
- Specialty groceries (organic, gluten-free): premium of ~$150–$200/month
- **Total: ~$450–$680/month** on things that aren't solving the root problem

Odin Lab at $750/month isn't an *add-on* — it's a *consolidation and upgrade*. The right framing: **"Stop buying pieces of a puzzle. Get the whole picture."**

### 2. The sunk cost of suffering is enormous
She has already spent:
- Years of reduced productivity at work
- Thousands on failed interventions
- Emotional cost of feeling broken, dismissed, and confused
- Relationship cost — she's not the partner or mother she wants to be

At this stage, $750/month feels *cheap* relative to another year of this. The ROI calculation in her head isn't "$750 vs. nothing" — it's "$750 vs. another year of feeling like this."

### 3. Health is a top-3 spending priority for her demographic
PwC research (2025): Women 31–50 control 75% of household discretionary spending and rank health as a top-3 priority category. The global women's wellness market is growing at 9.6% annually — driven precisely by this demographic.

She will cut subscriptions, dining out, or clothing before she cuts a health investment that's working.

### 4. She benchmarks against premium services she already buys
Emma already pays:
- $150–$200/month for a gym or studio membership
- $150–$300/session for a physio when she needs it
- $200–$400/year for dental hygiene
- $100–$200/session for a therapist or counsellor

A monthly health coaching membership at $750 sits in the same mental category as these — *professional expertise she pays for and doesn't question.* The key is that it must feel as credible and outcome-certain as those services.

### 5. The permission trigger: someone she trusts validates it
Emma rarely buys a high-ticket health service cold. Her most common path to purchase:
1. A friend or colleague mentions it worked for them *(word of mouth)*
2. She sees consistent content from a practitioner that makes her feel understood *(social proof + authority)*
3. She takes a quiz or assessment and gets a result that resonates *(the quiz does this)*
4. She has a discovery call where she feels *heard* for the first time *(the call closes it)*

The quiz → discovery call → membership is exactly the right funnel for this psychology.

### Owen's Buying Psychology

Owen is psychologically different from Emma in four critical ways:

**1. He doesn't research — he decides**
Emma spends 6 months researching before booking. Owen hears about it from a trusted source, looks at the website for 10 minutes, and either books or doesn't. His decision window is short. Don't make him read a long sales page. Give him: the problem (in his language), the solution (in specifics), the proof (testimonials from men like him), and the next step (a call, not a form).

**2. He's motivated by performance, not healing**
Emma wants to feel better. Owen wants to *perform* better. He doesn't identify as sick. He identifies as an optimiser who's found a bottleneck. Frame everything in output terms: energy, focus, body composition, testosterone levels, cognitive performance, sleep quality scores.

**3. He won't admit vulnerability publicly**
He won't post about his health on Facebook or join a support group. His referral path is private: a friend mentions it one-on-one over drinks or golf. He books quietly. He doesn't want his colleagues to know. **Confidentiality and professionalism in your marketing reassures him.**

**4. Price is not the objection — belief is**
He's not price sensitive. He'll pay $1,500/month without blinking if he believes it will work. The objection is: *"Is this legit or is it woo?"* The DUTCH test, functional labs, and the Ayurvedic science framed clinically (not spiritually) is what converts him. Lead with the science. The philosophy follows.
