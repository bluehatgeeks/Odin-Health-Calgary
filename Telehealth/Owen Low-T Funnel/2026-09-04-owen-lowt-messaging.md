## The Core Positioning (locked)

**The wedge:** Owen has already been failed twice — once by his doctor ("everything's normal, it's just age"), once by the internet (TRT gurus and random supplement stacks that didn't work). The funnel's job is to be the third, different voice: not dismissive, not reckless, not a shortcut.

**The three-way contrast** (this is the spine of every asset below):

| What Owen's tried / been told | Why it fails him | What Odin Labs offers instead |
|---|---|---|
| Doctor: "labs are normal, it's just age" | Standard panels don't test what's actually declining; dismissal, not diagnosis | A protocol built to find the actual pattern, not wave it off |
| Online gurus pushing TRT | Permanent, medicalizes something often reversible, real commitment/side-effect profile he hasn't fully reckoned with | For most men (we say ~80%), the issue is a fixable metabolic/adaptogen-responsive pattern — not a hormone-replacement-for-life situation |
| Random supplements off Amazon / TikTok | No structure, no dosing logic, no follow-up — he already tried this and it didn't move the needle | A protocol rooted in real science, sequenced, dosed, adjusted — not a bottle and a prayer |
| The little blue pill | Treats a symptom, not the cause; side effects (headaches) are real and undersold | Root-cause work — the downstream symptom stops needing a patch once the upstream pattern is addressed |

**The one non-negotiable belief to land before booking:** *this requires a professional, not another thing you try alone.* Every asset should reinforce this without sounding like a sales close — the point is that self-directed attempts (supplements, the pill) already failed specifically *because* they were unsupervised and generic, not because Owen didn't try hard enough.

### The most urgent angle: libido/ED, and why it's identity-level, not just symptom-level

Added 2026-09-04, research-backed (full sourcing in [[2026-09-04-andropause-low-t-belief-shifts.md]]): of everything in Owen's symptom basket, libido decline and erectile changes carry the most psychological weight, not just the most embarrassment. This is supported by real findings, not assumption:

- Peer-reviewed research on **"precarious manhood beliefs"** (the idea that manhood must be continually earned and can be lost) found these beliefs positively associated with ED itself — and men who feel they've lost their masculine identity because of ED are significantly more distressed by it *regardless of how clinically severe the ED actually is.* The belief is doing more damage than the biology.
- A 2021 survey found 65% of men reluctant to raise libido/fatigue with their doctor at all, and 45% felt discussing their health made them look weak — this symptom is the one most likely to go completely unspoken, including on this funnel's own quiz if it isn't asked about with care.
- A case-control study using standardized depression/self-esteem instruments confirmed the relationship is bidirectional: ED lowers self-esteem and raises depression risk, and existing distress worsens ED — it's a loop, not a static fact, which is itself a hopeful framing (loops can be interrupted; verdicts can't).

**The copy implication, and the one rule that matters most:** never frame this symptom as a referendum on masculinity ("get your manhood back," "feel like a man again") — the research says that framing reinforces the exact belief driving the distress and may worsen the dysfunction itself. Instead, fold it into the same fixable-pattern framing as everything else: not a separate, more shameful problem, just one more piece of the same addressable metabolic picture. This is the single highest-stakes copy decision in the whole funnel — get it right and it's the deepest belief-shift available; get it wrong and it actively works against the person most in need of a different answer.

## Symptom-First Language Bank

Adapted from Owen's avatar.md symptom basket + the client-language library's format conventions (verbatim-feel phrasing, dismissal-language capture, search-phrase structure). Written in Owen's voice — performance decline, not "symptoms."

### How Owen actually describes it (not clinical terms)

- "I used to be able to grind through anything. Now I'm gassing out by 3pm."
- "I fall asleep fine. I wake up at 3, 4am with my mind racing, and that's it — done for the night."
- "Same gym routine as five years ago. Different body. There's a gut now that wasn't there before."
- "I just... don't want to. Haven't told anyone that. Not my doctor, definitely not my wife."
- "I'm slower in meetings. Word retrieval's off. I catch myself making worse calls than I used to."
- "I snap at my kids over nothing. That's not me. Or it didn't used to be."
- "I used to bounce back from a bad week. Now a rough trip or a hard week takes days to recover from."

### What he's already been told (the dismissal — the wound)

- "Your labs are within normal range for your age."
- "This is just what happens when you turn 40 / 45 / 50."
- "Have you tried getting more sleep / exercising more / reducing stress?"
- "Testosterone's a little low but not low enough to treat."

### What he'd actually type into Google at 11pm

- "why am I so tired by 3pm every day"
- "waking up at 3am every night for no reason"
- "belly fat won't go away same diet same gym"
- "is low testosterone real or is it just age"
- "TRT vs natural testosterone boost"
- "testosterone supplements that actually work reddit"
- "why do I feel like this doctor says I'm fine"

### What he would never say (the dead expert-language version — do not lead with these)

- "andropause"
- "hypogonadism"
- "HPA-axis dysregulation"
- "cortisol dysregulation" (as a headline — fine as supporting detail once trust exists)
- "hormonal optimization protocol"

**Rule:** the gap between the "how he describes it" list and the "dead language" list is the whole game. Ad hooks, headlines, and Q1 of the quiz live in the first list. Clinical vocabulary is earned later — after the belief-shift class, inside the results/consult framing — never in the first thing he reads.

## Belief-Shift Structure

Adapted from the reference library's three-disbelief model (methodology / practitioner / self). For Owen, the dominant disbelief is **Self** — same tag as the closest analog entry — but reasoned independently: Owen's avatar.md establishes that price isn't his objection, *belief* is ("is this legit or is it woo?"), and that he's already tried and failed at self-directed fixes (supplements, possibly the pill). The failure mode isn't that he doubts the science exists — it's that he doubts *this attempt* will be different from his last two.

### The three disbeliefs standing between Owen and booking

**1. He doesn't believe the method will actually find what's wrong.** *(methodology)*
> His version: "I've tried supplements. I've read the Reddit threads. Everyone's selling something. How is this different from another guy with a stack to sell me?"

What he needs to believe: there's an actual assessment process — not a quiz that dumps him straight into a sales pitch, and not a generic protocol handed to every visitor.

**2. He doesn't believe a practitioner will take this seriously / treat it clinically.** *(practitioner/trust)*
> His version: "Is this legit, or is it woo? I don't want incense and a lecture about my chakras. I want someone who talks like my doctor, not like a wellness influencer."

What he needs to believe: this is delivered by a real clinician, framed clinically (labs, protocol, dosing logic), discreetly, professionally — not spiritually, not performatively.

**3. He doesn't believe *this time* will be different from the supplement stack and the little blue pill.** *(self — the loud one)*
> His version: "I've already spent money on this. Nothing moved. Why would this be different?"

What he needs to believe: the last two attempts failed for a specific, nameable reason (no structure, no diagnosis, no follow-up, treating a symptom instead of a cause) — not because "supplements don't work" or "he's too far gone." The fix isn't more willpower; it's a different kind of process.

### Trap vs. practitioner-version (do / don't)

**Ad hook / headline**
- ❌ TRAP: *"3 Herbs That Naturally Boost Testosterone — Buy Now"* (sounds like the exact supplement stack that already failed him)
- ✅ VERSION: *"Your doctor calls it aging. It's not age — it's a fixable metabolic pattern, and we'll show you which one is yours before you spend another dollar guessing."*

**Landing page / consult framing**
- ❌ TRAP: *"Your doctor missed the real reason you're exhausted. We didn't."* (undermines trust in his doctor in a way that reads as a sales tactic, not credibility)
- ✅ VERSION: *"Normal labs don't mean nothing's happening — they mean the standard panel isn't looking at what's actually shifted. We start with a real assessment, not a guess."*

**Email / DM opener**
- ❌ TRAP: *"Limited spots this month — don't wait until your testosterone drops further."* (urgency/scarcity — exactly what Video 2 flags as impulse-marketing that creates effort tourists)
- ✅ VERSION: *"Most men who come to us have already tried a supplement stack and gotten nowhere. That's usually not because supplements don't work — it's because nobody built a protocol around what was actually happening in their body first."*

**Offer line**
- ❌ TRAP: *"This adaptogen stack resets your cortisol in 30 days — no lifestyle changes needed."* (exactly the random-supplement promise the whole positioning is built to differentiate from)
- ✅ VERSION: *"This isn't a bottle you take and hope. It's a sequenced protocol — built around your actual pattern, adjusted as you go, with a clinician tracking what's working."*

## Applied Copy: Funnel Assets

### Ad hook options (symptom-first, no urgency, belief-building)

1. *"Your doctor says it's just age. Your last supplement stack said nothing at all. Here's what actually explains the 3pm crash."*
2. *"One side tells you TRT is dangerous. The other tells you every man over 35 should be on it. Neither one has actually looked at your pattern yet."*
3. *"You've tried the gym. You've tried the stack from Reddit. You've maybe tried the little blue pill and the headache that came with it. None of that was the wrong instinct — it was just missing a diagnosis."*
4. *"Normal labs. Abnormal energy. There's a reason those two things coexist, and it's not 'just age.'"*
5. *"Running on empty and the battery never charges — that's not a motivation problem. It's usually a pattern nobody's actually looked for."*
6. *"The thing you haven't mentioned to your doctor, or anyone — it's usually connected to the same pattern as the fatigue and the fog. Which means it's addressable the same way."* (identity-sensitive angle — see the dedicated section above before using; never pair with "manhood"/"real man" framing in the same asset or in surrounding creative)

### Landing page hero (pairs with the belief-shift mini-class)

**Headline:** *This Isn't Age. It's a Pattern — and Most of the Time, It's Fixable Without a Prescription.*

**Subhead:** *Your doctor ran the standard panel and called it normal. The internet sold you a stack, a pill, or a permanent hormone commitment. Before you pick a side, find out which one — if any — actually applies to you.*

**Belief-shift framing (before the quiz, matches mini-class content):**
> For roughly 80% of men dealing with what feels like andropause — the fatigue, the gut that won't move, the sleep that doesn't land, the drive that's gone quiet — the fix isn't testosterone replacement for life, and it isn't a bottle of ashwagandha from the pharmacy either. It's a structured, clinically-guided protocol built around what's actually happening in your body. This short video walks through why that distinction matters before you take the assessment below.

*(Mini-class script direction — not full script: open on the doctor-dismissal + guru-noise tension exactly as stated in the user's original brief; state the 80%-natural-resolution claim plainly; explicitly name why random supplements and the pill both fail as solutions — no structure/no diagnosis, and symptom-patch/side-effects respectively; land on "you need a protocol and you need a professional" as the single takeaway; end by pointing at the assessment below, no CTA pressure, no scarcity.)*

**Supporting research note (for the mini-class script and consult framing, not for headline claims):** the "rooted in science, not bro-science" positioning has real backing worth having in reserve. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials exist for both herbs named in the original protocol brief: a 90-day placebo-controlled trial of purified shilajit found significant increases in total testosterone, free testosterone, and DHEAS; a separate randomized double-blind trial of ashwagandha root extract found statistically significant improvements in erectile function scores (IIEF) and sexual health markers, plausibly mediated through ashwagandha's well-documented cortisol-lowering effect. This is genuine clinical-trial evidence, not supplement-industry marketing — use it to earn the "science, not guessing" claim honestly, but keep it downstream of the belief-shift class (per the project outline, no protocol/dosing specifics pre-call) and never overstate it into a guarantee.

### Quiz Q1 (symptom-first entry, not clinical)

Instead of: *"Have you been diagnosed with low testosterone or andropause?"*

Use: *"Which of these sounds like your last month? (pick all that apply)"*
- Gassing out by mid-afternoon, every day
- Falling asleep fine, waking up at 2-4am and staying awake
- A gut/midsection that wasn't there a few years ago, same diet and gym routine
- Changes in drive or performance you haven't mentioned to anyone — not your doctor, not your partner
- Slower in meetings, more irritable at home, less patience than you used to have

**Note on the drive/performance option's wording:** deliberately not clinical ("erectile dysfunction," "libido") and deliberately not crude. "Changes ... you haven't mentioned to anyone" does two jobs at once — it's specific enough that a man dealing with this recognizes himself immediately, and it names the silence around it directly, which per the belief-shifts research is itself a relief (someone naming the unspoken thing, without making it the headline). This option should never be visually or contextually separated from the others as more significant/serious-looking — per the belief-shift rule, it is one item in the same list, not a flagged special case.

### Results page framing (matches Owen's existing two-step mechanic)

**Step 1 (collapsed by default) — pattern reveal:**
> Based on your answers, your pattern points toward [outcome bucket]. This is a pattern, not a diagnosis — a clinician needs to interpret it against your actual history before it means anything actionable.

**Step 2 (expanded, booking widget) — belief-reinforcing CTA:**
> You've likely already tried fixing this on your own — a supplement stack, maybe more sleep, maybe the gym harder. If none of that moved the needle, that's not a willpower problem. It's a structure problem. Book your free 15-minute call and a clinician will walk through what your pattern actually means and whether a natural protocol is the right first step for you — before anyone talks about TRT.
